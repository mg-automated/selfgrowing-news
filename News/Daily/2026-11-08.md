Diplomatic and political pressure intensified across several fronts as Russia named a senior military-diplomatic team for renewed Ukraine talks, Israel's president gave Gadi Eisenkot the first attempt to form a government and Donald Trump directed the US Justice Department to investigate Democratic threats of oversight. ## 1. Russia names senior delegation for renewed Ukraine peace talks

Russia said Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky will lead a delegation of senior diplomats and military officials to US-mediated talks with Ukraine in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday. The team includes Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov, military intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov and General Staff officer Yuri Zhigarlovsky, and the meeting will be the first trilateral talks since February.

**Why it matters:** The delegation's seniority and mix of diplomatic and operational officials give the renewed negotiations political weight, although no agreement or breakthrough is assured.

## 2. Eisenkot receives first mandate to form Israel's next government

Israeli President Isaac Herzog chose former military chief Gadi Eisenkot to try to form a government after the October 27 election left no bloc with a parliamentary majority. Eisenkot has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, to assemble a coalition after receiving endorsements from parties holding more seats than those nominating Benjamin Netanyahu.

**Why it matters:** The mandate opens a difficult path to ending Israel's post-election deadlock and could determine whether Netanyahu remains in office after his coalition lost its majority.

**Topics:** [Israel](../../Topics/Israel.md), [Palestinian Politics](../../Topics/Palestinian-Politics.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-president-picks-gadi-eisenkot-netanyahu-rival-form-government-2026-11-07/)

## 3. Poland begins mourning after Krakow mass shooting

A gunman killed six people and wounded 17 others in Krakow's Main Market Square before police shot and arrested him, according to Polish authorities. Officials said early evidence suggested far-right influence, while three more bodies believed to be members of the suspect's family were found at his home; President Karol Nawrocki declared November 8 and 9 days of national mourning.

**Why it matters:** The unprecedented attack is likely to intensify scrutiny of firearms licensing, online extremism and domestic security in Poland.

**Topics:** [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/two-kiled-least-10-injured-shooting-polish-city-krakow-tvn24-reports-2026-11-07/)

## 4. Trump directs Justice Department to examine Democratic oversight threats

President Donald Trump ordered the Justice Department to look into Democrats whom he accused, without providing evidence, of criminally threatening people with impeachment and other punishment. The directive follows Democratic victories in both chambers of Congress and promised investigations into administration actions when the new Congress begins in January.

**Why it matters:** The order raises concerns that federal law enforcement could be drawn into the president's struggle with an incoming opposition-controlled Congress.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-orders-doj-look-into-democrats-threatening-people-2026-11-07/)

## 5. US House Democrats set healthcare and Iran war as early priorities

House Democratic leaders said their new majority will begin with votes to restore enhanced Affordable Care Act subsidies, reverse Medicaid cuts and seek an end to the US war with Iran. They also plan measures on tariffs, living costs, artificial intelligence and data centres, although Senate rules and a possible presidential veto could block enactment.

**Why it matters:** The agenda shows how Democrats intend to convert their midterm victory into legislative pressure on the Trump administration even under divided government.

## 6. Italy investigates leak of foreign minister's personal data

Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said his personal data had been published on the dark web and that criminals were seeking further information about him and his family. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni blamed pro-Russian hackers, while Tajani linked the targeting to Italy's support for Ukraine; neither official disclosed the leaked material or public evidence identifying the attackers.

**Why it matters:** Targeting a senior minister and his family illustrates how cyber operations can combine data theft, intimidation and geopolitical pressure below the threshold of conventional conflict.

## 7. Swiss committee backs CHF193 million Erasmus+ contribution

The House of Representatives' Foreign Affairs Committee recommended approving Switzerland's CHF193 million contribution to the Erasmus+ exchange programme for 2027 by 16 votes to nine. The Senate has already approved the credit, while the committee continues separate deliberations on the wider Switzerland-EU treaty package.

**Why it matters:** The vote advances Switzerland's return to European education exchanges and forms part of the broader political effort to stabilise relations with the European Union.

## 8. Swiss sustainable-fund assets reach record CHF1.36 trillion

Assets in sustainable funds available in Switzerland rose 11% to a record CHF1.363 trillion, according to a study by the Lucerne University of Applied Sciences and Arts. The increase was driven mainly by capital gains rather than new investor money, while the number of funds fell from 2,038 to 1,900 amid closures and stricter naming rules.

**Why it matters:** The record headline masks weak inflows and product consolidation, showing how regulation and market performance are reshaping Switzerland's sustainable-finance sector.

**Topics:** [Swiss Banking Regulation](../../Topics/Swiss-Banking-Regulation.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/various/1363-billion-swiss-francs-in-sustainable-funds/92343235)

## 9. US election results strengthen pressure for data-centre restrictions

An Associated Press review found that 32 of 34 winning gubernatorial candidates it tracked supported stronger regulation or limits on data centres, and voters approved restrictions in 44 of 45 completed local referendums. Incoming officials are considering disclosure rules, local approval requirements, utility-cost protections and the removal of tax incentives as communities challenge the electricity and water demands of AI infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** State and local policy could become the main constraint on US AI infrastructure growth even if federal legislation stalls under divided government.

**Topics:** [AI Infrastructure](../../Topics/AI-Infrastructure.md), [AI Governance](../../Topics/AI-Governance.md), [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/63b33be8884d867d3ac831040d683a2c)

## 10. Environmental groups confront AI's climate trade-offs

Reuters found that all 15 environmental, conservation and development organisations it interviewed use AI while also expressing concern about the energy, water and emissions footprint of the data centres powering it. Groups are applying AI to wildfire alerts, wildlife counts and historical research, while some limit resource-intensive uses, cache results or process data on local devices to reduce environmental costs.

**Why it matters:** The tension illustrates how organisations pursuing climate and biodiversity goals must weigh faster analysis against the infrastructure footprint of the tools they adopt.
