## 1. NATO deploys early-warning aircraft to Poland after Russian missiles land there

NATO authorised the forward deployment of an AWACS early-warning aircraft to Poland after Polish prosecutors said three Russian Kh-101 missiles had landed in the country. The alliance's supreme commander is also assessing whether additional assets are needed along the eastern flank.

**Why it matters:** The deployment strengthens NATO's ability to detect and coordinate responses to airborne threats while underscoring the risk that Russia's war could spill into alliance territory.

## 2. Ukraine acknowledges sinking a Russian warship in Libya

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian maritime drones sank the Russian corvette *Merkury* at Benghazi naval base and struck the military-linked cargo ship *MV Angara*. Reuters verified footage showing the attacks, while rival Libyan authorities condemned the operation and said their territory must not become an arena for the Russia–Ukraine war.

**Why it matters:** Kyiv's first acknowledgement of an operation in Libya shows that its campaign against Russian military logistics can reach far beyond Europe and creates new escalation risks in the Mediterranean.

**Topics:** [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

## 3. Dozens reported killed in retaliatory attacks in Nigeria's Niger state

Fulani community representatives said 64 people were killed in attacks around Wawa and Gada-Oli after five people, including a village chief, were killed during morning prayers in a nearby settlement. Police had not issued a toll, and Reuters could not independently verify the community reports or identify the attackers.

**Why it matters:** The reported reprisals risk intensifying communal violence, displacement and insecurity in an area already affected by armed attacks, kidnappings and cattle theft.

**Topics:** [Nigeria](../../Topics/Nigeria.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/dozens-killed-weekend-attacks-nigerias-niger-state-residents-say-2026-11-23/)

## 4. WFP warns war-related shipping disruption could cut aid to 7.3 million more people

World Food Programme chief Luke Lindberg said disruption at the Black Sea, Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb could add $727 million to the agency's costs by mid-2027. He urged diplomats to revive a humanitarian grain corridor covering both Ukrainian and Russian exports, warning that some 10 million tonnes of grain could otherwise fail to reach global markets this year.

**Why it matters:** Higher transport and food costs could force the world's largest humanitarian agency to reduce assistance further when 266 million people already face acute hunger.

## 5. Denmark's Supreme Court upholds evictions under its hard-line integration law

Denmark's Supreme Court ruled that a housing association did not directly discriminate when it ended 91 leases to prevent a neighbourhood from being classified under the country's former “hard ghetto” category. The decision upheld a lower-court ruling despite an earlier EU court finding that the law could amount to ethnic discrimination depending on how national courts assessed its effects.

**Why it matters:** The judgment preserves a central element of Denmark's influential hard-line integration policy and sharpens the legal debate over national discretion and EU anti-discrimination standards.

## 6. Swiss Greens nominate a challenger for Parmelin's Federal Council seat

The Green parliamentary group nominated Geneva National Councillor Delphine Klopfenstein Broggini for the seat being vacated by Economics Minister Guy Parmelin. The party argued that the government should retain regional and gender balance and criticised the Swiss People's Party's two-man, German-speaking ticket.

**Why it matters:** The candidacy gives parliament an alternative in the government election and turns the succession into a broader contest over party strength, language-region representation and concordance.

## 7. Switzerland and China hold fifth strategic dialogue

Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis met Chinese counterpart Wang Yi in Nanjing to discuss bilateral relations, trade, research cooperation and international issues including Ukraine and the Korean Peninsula. The talks followed the signing of an upgraded free-trade agreement and a framework for handling scientific data across borders.

**Why it matters:** Switzerland is deepening economic and diplomatic engagement with China while navigating European trade tensions, data restrictions and major security disputes.

## 8. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as lawmakers demand tougher China-chip controls

Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive after determining that he posed no national-security risk, reversing his departure earlier in November. Bipartisan US lawmakers criticised the decision, renewed calls for chip-location tracking and export-control legislation, and said Huang is still expected to appear before House panels on December 1.

**Why it matters:** Huang's return steadies the world's dominant AI-chip supplier but intensifies congressional scrutiny of Nvidia's governance and the diversion of advanced processors to China.

## 9. CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion

CrowdStrike agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at about $43.9 billion, the largest cybersecurity acquisition on record. The combination would bring endpoint, network and zero-trust security into one platform and begin with more than $10 billion in combined annual recurring revenue.

**Why it matters:** The deal would reshape the enterprise-security market as customers consolidate suppliers and AI-driven attacks increase demand for protection across devices, networks and autonomous agents.

## 10. Australia elevates artificial intelligence to a foreign-policy priority

Foreign Minister Penny Wong said Australia will treat AI as a central foreign-policy challenge alongside US–China competition and the growing influence of middle powers. The government plans to strengthen critical-technology resilience and frontier-model safeguards while working with partners on international AI safety and foreign-interference risks.

**Why it matters:** Australia's approach explicitly links AI governance with diplomacy, democratic resilience and strategic power, adding momentum to international coordination on frontier systems.
