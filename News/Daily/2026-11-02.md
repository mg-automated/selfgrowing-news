# Daily News — 2026-11-02

Coverage window: 2026-11-01 05:48:13 to 2026-11-02 05:48:13 (Europe/Zurich)

## Overview

Limited diplomatic and humanitarian openings remained fragile: the Oleshky ceasefire ended after successful evacuations, US–Iran mediation continued, and the UN began closing its long-running Western Sahara mission after Security Council deadlock. Fighting and coercion persisted through large energy attacks in Ukraine, new tanker explosions in the Strait of Hormuz and expanded Chinese coast-guard activity around Taiwan. Elsewhere, election litigation tested US federal authority, unrest reached Swiss cities, and Asian institutions accelerated investment in embodied and financial artificial intelligence.

## 1. UN begins closing its Western Sahara peacekeeping mission

The United Nations began winding down its 35-year-old MINURSO mission after the Security Council failed to renew its mandate. Russia vetoed a US-backed resolution favouring Morocco's autonomy plan, while a competing Russian proposal also failed, leaving the mission that monitored the 1991 ceasefire and was meant to organise a referendum without legal authority to continue.

**Why it matters:** Removing the peacekeeping presence eliminates an established ceasefire-monitoring mechanism while the territory's political status remains unresolved.

## 2. Oleshky ceasefire expires after nearly 800 people are evacuated

A six-day local ceasefire around Russian-held Oleshky expired after both sides fulfilled commitments that enabled mine clearance, aid deliveries and the evacuation of nearly 800 people. Ukraine separately evacuated 167 people from other threatened parts of Kherson region, and the Ukrainian and Russian human-rights commissioners said they were exploring further humanitarian cooperation.

**Why it matters:** The completed operation shows that narrow agreements can protect civilians even while a comprehensive ceasefire remains distant.

**Topics:** [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/ukraine-evacuates-civilians-parts-kherson-region-expiry-oleshky-ceasefire-nears-2026-11-01/)

## 3. Russian strikes cut power to hundreds of thousands in Ukraine

Russian attacks hit electricity infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions and left hundreds of thousands of households without power, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskiy. Ukraine said it struck a Russian plant involved in missile production, while Russian authorities reported significant damage to an industrial facility after a large drone attack and Moscow said it intercepted 235 Ukrainian drones.

**Why it matters:** The exchange shows that energy and industrial infrastructure remain central targets despite proposals for narrower pauses ahead of winter.

**Topics:** [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/russian-strikes-cut-power-thousands-ukraine-drones-damage-russian-facility-2026-11-01/)

## 4. Iran and Qatar hold a third day of mediated talks

Iranian negotiators and Qatari mediators continued talks in Doha for a third consecutive day, with the discussions coordinated through contacts with Washington. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said consultations on a proposal that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days were progressing, but no final conclusion had been reached.

**Why it matters:** Sustained mediation keeps a diplomatic route open immediately before the US midterm elections, after which the risk of renewed military escalation may rise.

**Topics:** [Iran](../../Topics/Iran.md), [Strait of Hormuz](../../Topics/Strait-of-Hormuz.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iranian-negotiators-doha-continue-talks-end-us-iran-war-official-says-2026-11-01/)

## 5. Iran says two tankers exploded after striking mines in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said two oil tankers exploded after hitting mines while travelling through what it called an unauthorised route in the Strait of Hormuz. The statement followed repeated attacks and Iranian restrictions on shipping through the strategic waterway, although Reuters did not report independent confirmation of the latest incidents.

**Why it matters:** Fresh tanker explosions undermine negotiations over reopening the Strait and sustain risks to crews, oil supplies and global energy prices.

**Topics:** [Strait of Hormuz](../../Topics/Strait-of-Hormuz.md), [Iran](../../Topics/Iran.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/irans-irgc-navy-says-two-tankers-exploded-after-hitting-mines-hormuz-2026-11-01/)

## 6. US judge dismisses federal attempt to block an Alaska election result

A federal judge dismissed the Trump administration's lawsuit seeking to prevent Alaska from certifying a possible Senate victory by Democrat Mary Peltola if votes for a second candidate named Daniel Sullivan affected the result. The judge ruled that the federal government lacked standing and that its proposed ballot segregation could violate voter secrecy; the Justice Department said it would appeal.

**Why it matters:** The decision limits an attempted expansion of federal authority over a closely contested state-run election that could help determine control of the Senate.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-judge-throws-out-trump-election-lawsuit-alaska-2026-11-01/)

## 7. Taiwan accuses China of expanding coast-guard coercion

Taiwan said two Chinese coast-guard ships patrolling its east coast were harassing shipping and asserting jurisdiction that Beijing does not possess, prompting Taipei to deploy its own vessels. Taiwan also reported another Chinese coast-guard ship near the Taiwan-controlled Pratas Islands, which sit on strategically important approaches to the South China Sea.

**Why it matters:** Regularised coast-guard patrols could help China practise blockade tactics below the threshold of conventional military action.

## 8. Chinese robotics investment shifts toward embodied AI

Chinese companies are increasingly investing in embodied-AI software intended to help robots understand environments, learn unfamiliar tasks and adapt across workplaces. Funding in the sector reached 93.5 billion yuan across 322 deals in the first half of 2026, five times the year-earlier total, although reliable performance outside controlled settings remains difficult.

**Why it matters:** Combining China's manufacturing scale with more capable robot intelligence could reshape industrial automation and open a new front in US–China technology competition.

## 9. HSBC plans a Hong Kong AI institute as regulator develops agent safeguards

HSBC said it plans to open an artificial-intelligence and emerging-technology institute in Hong Kong in early 2027, working with the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority separately announced Project SAGE to test financial AI agents and examine governance, accountability, a trusted-agent register and a possible “know your agent” standard.

**Why it matters:** The paired initiatives show financial institutions and regulators trying to scale AI adoption while building controls for autonomous systems that can act across banking processes.

## 10. Unauthorised protests in Zurich and Basel turn violent

Several hundred people joined unauthorised demonstrations in Zurich and Basel inspired by youth-led protests elsewhere in Europe. Police in Zurich used water cannon, tear gas and rubber bullets after fires, property damage and attacks on officers, while barricades were also set alight in Basel.

**Why it matters:** The clashes extend a wider European protest movement into Switzerland and will test how local authorities balance public order with political expression.
