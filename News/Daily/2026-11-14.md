# Daily News — 2026-11-14

Coverage window: 2026-11-13 05:41 to 2026-11-14 05:41 (Europe/Zurich)

## Overview

Diplomacy and technology converged as the United States prepared an unprecedented meeting with the leaders of China and Russia, ASEAN opened talks amid energy and security pressures, and European leaders held their first dedicated AI summit. Major technology developments included OpenAI's agent-payment wallet, evidence of China's continuing reliance on restricted Nvidia chips, and two large infrastructure failures, while Swiss debates focused on defence-industry rules, wages and an upcoming Qatari state visit.

## 1. Trump plans nuclear-arms talks with Xi and Putin in China

The White House said US President Donald Trump will meet Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in China on November 19, on the sidelines of the APEC conference in Shenzhen. Trump intends to raise reductions in nuclear weapons, although Beijing has resisted trilateral arms-control talks because the United States and Russia possess much larger arsenals.

**Why it matters:** A first meeting of the three leaders could shape strategic stability, but the gap between their nuclear forces and priorities makes a quick agreement unlikely.

## 2. ASEAN opens summit diplomacy under energy and security pressure

Southeast Asian foreign ministers began meetings in Manila before the November 15–17 ASEAN summit, where leaders are expected to discuss high fuel costs, Myanmar, the South China Sea and transboundary pollution. The Philippines said negotiations on a South China Sea code of conduct were about 70% complete overall, while Myanmar's exclusion from leaders' meetings remained contested.

**Why it matters:** ASEAN's ability to maintain common positions is being tested simultaneously by economic strain and conflicts that divide its members and major-power partners.

**Topics:** [Pacific Security](../../Topics/Pacific-Security.md), [Myanmar](../../Topics/Myanmar.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/asean-unity-faces-test-energy-crisis-regional-conflicts-loom-over-summit-2026-11-13/)

## 3. European Union leaders hold their first dedicated AI summit

EU leaders met in Dublin for their first summit focused specifically on artificial intelligence, debating investment, regulation, safety and Europe's ability to compete with the United States and China. The discussion included stalled AI-gigafactory plans and concerns that the EU Chips Act may miss its manufacturing targets; leaders also discussed Ukraine and possible European security guarantees.

**Why it matters:** The summit moves AI competitiveness and safety to the highest political level as Europe struggles to turn policy ambitions into infrastructure and industrial capacity.

## 4. OpenAI launches a wallet for purchases made by AI agents

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which allows AI agents to pay for purchases without accessing users' card details. The company said every transaction requires a security check, but the initial announcement provided little detail on supported merchants, payment methods or availability.

**Why it matters:** Giving agents payment capability extends them from recommending actions to executing financial transactions, increasing both their practical value and the need for reliable consent and fraud controls.

## 5. Chinese AI projects remain heavily dependent on restricted Nvidia chips

A Reuters review identified more than 150 Chinese projects and equipment agreements worth over 23 billion yuan ($3.2 billion) that were designed around restricted Nvidia H100, H200 or H800 processors. Some facilities are operating or under construction, while others face delays or expensive redesigns as US export controls tighten.

**Why it matters:** The projects show both the persistence of Chinese demand for Nvidia technology and the difficulty of enforcing export controls across complex infrastructure and procurement chains.

## 6. Global Microsoft outage disrupts governments and businesses

A server-related Microsoft outage disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot for more than 12 hours, slowing communications and administrative work at businesses and public authorities worldwide. Microsoft said it had found no known malicious activity and reported signs of stabilisation after broader fixes, while affected organisations resorted to phones, Bluetooth and even fax machines.

**Why it matters:** The outage exposed the systemic risk created when governments and companies depend on a small number of cloud and communications providers.

## 7. Communications failure grounds departing flights across the United States

The Federal Aviation Administration imposed a nationwide ground stop after communications failed at air-traffic-control centres, causing more than 1,000 cancellations and nearly 10,000 delays. Homeland Security said there was no indication of a cyberattack, while officials investigated possible causes and the disruption spread to flights from Canada and Europe.

**Why it matters:** Only the second nationwide US ground stop since September 11, 2001, demonstrated how ageing critical infrastructure can create rapid international disruption.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md), [Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Cybersecurity.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/faa-issues-national-ground-stop-due-extraordinary-circumstance-reasons-safety-2026-11-13/)

## 8. Swiss defence companies challenge the reach of anti-mercenary law

Swiss defence and technology companies said the Federal Act on Private Security Services Provided Abroad complicates installing and maintaining military equipment and training foreign personnel. Industry representatives argued that its reach over foreign subsidiaries and support work is driving contracts and potentially headquarters abroad, while the foreign ministry said parliament deliberately designed broad coverage and most declarations are processed within days.

**Why it matters:** The dispute pits Switzerland's safeguards against private military activity against its effort to retain a viable defence industry during European rearmament.

## 9. Swiss trade unions prepare an initiative for a CHF5,000 skilled-worker wage

Delegates of the Swiss Trade Union Federation approved developing a popular initiative that would give workers who completed an apprenticeship a minimum monthly wage of CHF5,000 for full-time employment. The draft also proposes eight weeks of annual holiday for apprentices; the federation says nearly three in ten qualified workers currently earn below the proposed threshold.

**Why it matters:** If launched and approved, the proposal would establish a national wage floor tied to vocational qualifications and significantly reshape Swiss employment policy.

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/various/the-uss-wants-a-minimum-wage-following-an-apprenticeship/92376717)

## 10. Qatar's Amir will make a state visit to Switzerland

The Swiss government announced that Qatar's Amir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani will make a two-day state visit to Switzerland in the coming week at the invitation of the Federal Council. Bern described the invitation as recognition of growing relations with Qatar and the Arabian Peninsula.

**Why it matters:** The visit gives Switzerland a high-level channel to deepen bilateral ties with a state playing an important diplomatic and economic role in the Middle East.
