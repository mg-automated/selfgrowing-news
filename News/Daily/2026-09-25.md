## 1. US and Iran explore a phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

US and Iranian negotiators in New York discussed a phased arrangement under which Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and Washington would lift its economic blockade, according to sources close to the talks. Both sides were reluctant to surrender their leverage first, and Iran signalled flexibility over transit fees without yielding control of the strait.

**Why it matters:** A workable sequence could begin ending a months-long war and restore traffic through a route central to global energy supplies, but the order of concessions remains a major obstacle.

**Why it matters:** Activating a new defence partnership around the Houthi threat could widen regional military coordination and alter the balance of the Yemen conflict.

## 3. Abbas pledges the first Palestinian parliamentary election in 20 years

Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas reaffirmed plans for legislative elections on November 28, which would be the first such vote in two decades. Divisions within Fatah, the difficulty of voting in Gaza and Hamas's effort to assemble a rival coalition all cast doubt on whether the election will proceed as announced.

**Why it matters:** A credible election could renew the Palestinian leadership's democratic mandate, but failure to hold an inclusive vote would deepen an already severe legitimacy crisis.

## 4. Russian drone strike kills a child in central Kyiv

A Russian drone struck a 16-storey residential building in Kyiv's central Pechersk district, killing a 14-year-old boy and injuring at least seven people, Ukrainian officials said. A separate attack killed another person in the southern Odesa region.

**Why it matters:** The strike underscores the continuing danger to civilians in major Ukrainian cities despite renewed discussion of limited ceasefires and winter protections.

## 6. Swiss Senate votes to narrow protection for journalistic sources

The Council of States adopted a motion by 34 votes to 10 that would limit source protection to media professionals for whom it is directly necessary to safeguard media freedom. The proposal would also narrow protection against seizure and now goes to the National Council.

**Why it matters:** Relaxing source confidentiality could make official-secrecy cases easier to prosecute but may deter whistleblowers and weaken investigative journalism.

## 7. Swiss Senate seeks tighter rules for use of the Swiss cross

The Council of States approved a motion by 32 votes to 5 calling for the Swiss cross to be barred from products manufactured abroad. Senators said a March policy change allowing the symbol alongside “Swiss Engineering” encouraged offshoring and disadvantaged companies that manufacture domestically; the National Council must still consider the motion.

**Why it matters:** The proposal would reshape how Switzerland protects the commercial value of national branding and balance domestic production against research and design performed in Switzerland.

**Topics:** [Swiss Product Regulation](../../Topics/Swiss-Product-Regulation.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/global-trade/no-swiss-cross-for-products-manufactured-abroad/92113950)

## 8. Oracle data-centre delay rattles AI infrastructure financing

Oracle issued a force-majeure notice over potential power delays at Blue Owl's Project Jupiter data centre in New Mexico, while people familiar with the deal said the project could face a one-year delay. Blue Owl has about $3 billion in equity invested, and the dispute unsettled financiers confronting an estimated $1.5 trillion need for external AI-infrastructure funding by 2028.

**Why it matters:** The episode shows how grid access and contractual risk can constrain the enormous capital build-out required for frontier AI, even when investor demand remains strong.

## 10. Google prepares the first orbital test of its AI chips

Google said it would launch a prototype satellite carrying Tensor Processing Units in its first in-orbit test of Project Suncatcher. The mission will examine whether the chips can withstand launch forces, radiation, extreme temperatures and vacuum cooling conditions, while later missions are intended to test laser links between computing satellites.

**Why it matters:** Orbital computing could eventually use abundant solar power to ease terrestrial energy constraints on AI, but the test also highlights the formidable engineering and cost barriers.
