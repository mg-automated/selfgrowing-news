# Daily News Briefing — 2026-11-26

Coverage window: 2026-11-25 05:46 to 2026-11-26 05:46 (Europe/Zurich)

## Overview

Ukraine expanded its campaign against Russian military aviation as North Korea accelerated its nuclear programme and closed a newly reopened Swiss diplomatic office. Switzerland also faced new US tariffs, took another step toward its EU treaty package and proposed narrower corporate due-diligence rules, while record migration arrivals put pressure on Spain before an election. In technology, cyber incidents disrupted US financial-crime systems and prompted a major Microsoft reliability overhaul, as Anthropic prepared a larger research presence in Zurich.

## 1. Ukraine says strike damaged five aircraft at major Russian air base

Ukraine's General Staff said its forces struck Savasleyka air base east of Moscow, temporarily disabling three MiG-31 aircraft used to launch Kinzhal hypersonic missiles and two Su-34 fighter-bombers. Russia did not immediately comment, and the extent of the reported damage was still being verified.

**Why it matters:** The operation extends Kyiv's long-range campaign from Russian energy and logistics infrastructure to aircraft directly involved in attacks on Ukrainian cities.

## 2. North Korea orders wider deployment of tactical nuclear weapons

Kim Jong Un called for tactical nuclear weapons to be used across a wider range of missions while inspecting nuclear-material production and weapons-research facilities, state media reported. He said production capacity was expanding systematically and described North Korea's nuclear status as irreversible.

**Why it matters:** The directive signals further growth and diversification of Pyongyang's arsenal despite renewed US outreach and increasing regional concern about its enrichment capacity.

## 3. Canary Islands record more than 2,000 migrant arrivals in one day

More than 2,100 migrants reached Spain's Canary Islands in overcrowded boats over 24 hours, the highest single-day total recorded on the Atlantic route. Maritime rescue data showed annual arrivals had risen above 28,000, with the surge occurring days before Spain's national election.

**Why it matters:** The record exposes the humanitarian danger and reception pressure along one of the world's deadliest migration routes while placing migration at the centre of Spain's election campaign.

**Topics:** [Spain](../../Topics/Spain.md), [European Migration](../../Topics/European-Migration.md)

## 4. North Korea expels Swiss diplomat and closes Pyongyang office

North Korea declared the head of Switzerland's diplomatic office persona non grata and ordered him to leave within 48 hours, less than a month after the office reopened. Pyongyang linked the decision to Swiss sanctions on a children's camp accused of involvement in transfers of Ukrainian children, while Bern said diplomatic relations would continue through the two countries' embassies in Beijing and Bern.

**Why it matters:** The closure removes a rare direct diplomatic channel and shows how enforcement of sanctions connected to Russia's war is constraining Switzerland's mediation role.

## 5. United States announces new tariffs of up to 15% on Swiss goods

The US Trade Representative proposed additional tariffs of up to 15% on selected Swiss imports after finding that Swiss industrial and currency policies contributed to structural overcapacity. Switzerland rejected the allegations and said it expected Washington to respect the 2025 declaration that capped the overall tariff rate at 15%.

**Why it matters:** The measures threaten Swiss exporters and reopen trade friction with Washington at a time when pharmaceuticals, chemicals and precision manufacturing remain central to the bilateral relationship.

## 6. European Parliament approves Swiss–EU treaty package

The European Parliament endorsed the package of agreements with Switzerland by 584 votes to 13, with 60 abstentions. The package updates existing bilateral arrangements and adds agreements covering electricity, health and food safety, but still requires completion of Switzerland's domestic approval process.

**Why it matters:** The vote removes a major hurdle on the EU side and shifts attention to Swiss parliamentary debate and the likely referendum over the country's long-term relationship with its largest trading partner.

**Topics:** [Swiss EU Relations](../../Topics/Swiss-EU-Relations.md), [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/foreign-affairs/eu-parliament-approves-the-switzerland-eu-treaty-package/92436424)

## 7. Swiss government proposes targeted corporate due-diligence law

The Federal Council adopted an indirect counter-proposal to the responsible-business initiative that would impose risk-based human-rights and environmental duties on roughly 30 to 60 very large Swiss companies. The proposal uses fault-based liability rather than automatic liability for business partners and reduces potential sanctions compared with the consultation draft.

**Why it matters:** The bill seeks a compromise between corporate accountability and competitiveness and will give parliament an alternative to the broader popular initiative.

**Topics:** [Swiss Direct Democracy](../../Topics/Swiss-Direct-Democracy.md), [Swiss Product Regulation](../../Topics/Swiss-Product-Regulation.md)

## 8. US financial-crime agency takes systems offline after cyber incident

The US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network took some systems offline after unauthorised access to a limited part of its network. Officials said there was no evidence that Bank Secrecy Act or beneficial-ownership databases were breached, while federal agencies and external specialists continued investigating the scope.

**Why it matters:** Disruption at the agency responsible for receiving financial-intelligence reports could impede anti-money-laundering work and adds to a series of cyber incidents across sensitive US government systems.

## 9. Microsoft launches “Code Red” reliability overhaul after historic outage

Microsoft declared its highest-priority “Code Red” programme after a 24-hour outage disrupted Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other services worldwide. Internal memos reviewed by Reuters said the company is targeting global recovery within one hour and improving traffic isolation, diagnostics and emergency patch deployment.

**Why it matters:** The response acknowledges structural weaknesses in infrastructure used by governments and businesses and shows how concentrated cloud services can turn a software defect into a global operational crisis.

## 10. Anthropic prepares major expansion of its Zurich research base

Anthropic is reportedly leasing about 2,500 square metres in Zurich's former Fraumünster post office, with capacity for roughly 150 workstations. The company plans to conduct research and contribute to model training from Switzerland, although it has not disclosed how many people it will hire.

**Why it matters:** The expansion strengthens Zurich's position as a European AI research centre and intensifies competition for specialists around ETH Zurich and the city's established technology sector.
