## 1. Zelenskyy questions Russia's willingness to compromise in renewed peace process

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he remained more sceptical than US officials that Vladimir Putin was ready to compromise, despite Washington's efforts to organise another round of talks. He welcomed European participation but said Russia was likely to delay the process, while reiterating that Ukraine would not surrender the entire Donbas region.

**Why it matters:** The gap over territory and timing remains a central obstacle to converting renewed US-mediated diplomacy into a durable settlement.

## 2. States extend autonomous-weapons talks without agreeing binding limits

The 128 parties to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons agreed in Geneva to spend two years elaborating a possible international instrument for lethal autonomous weapons systems. The consensus mandate stopped short of the legally binding restrictions sought by the UN secretary-general, the International Committee of the Red Cross and many governments, after opposition from major military powers.

**Why it matters:** The outcome keeps multilateral regulation alive but leaves weapons capable of selecting and attacking targets without firm international limits during a period of rapid military-AI development.

## 3. EU leaders' chair proposes deeper budget cuts and a digital tax

European Council President António Costa proposed reducing the European Union's 2028–2034 budget to €1.582 trillion, cutting competitiveness, defence and external spending while protecting agriculture and cohesion comparatively more. He also suggested revenue from a digital tax and slower repayment of jointly issued recovery debt ahead of a special leaders' summit on 26–27 November.

**Why it matters:** The proposal tests whether EU governments can finance defence, industrial policy and debt repayment without widening national contributions or provoking a trade clash with the United States.

**Topics:** [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/eu-leaders-chair-proposes-new-cuts-eu-2028-2034-budget-draft-suggests-digital-2026-11-21/)

## 4. COP31 negotiators approach a final deal after talks run into overtime

The Australian and Turkish presidencies of COP31 presented a new package in Antalya as negotiations continued beyond their scheduled close. Delegations remained divided over fossil fuels and the strength of the final text, with the European Union warning earlier that it preferred no agreement to a weak one.

**Why it matters:** The final package will indicate whether governments can strengthen collective climate action despite widening disputes over energy security, finance and the transition away from fossil fuels.

## 5. Democrats prepare an ambitious agenda after winning Congress

Democrats are preparing a first-100-days programme before taking control of both chambers on 3 January, with priorities expected to include healthcare, voting rights and oversight of the Trump administration. Their 56-seat Senate majority still falls short of the 60 votes normally needed to advance most legislation, while progressives are pressing for measures including a moratorium on new AI data centres.

**Why it matters:** The size of the Democratic victory creates room for major policy changes, but Senate procedure and internal divisions will determine how much of the agenda becomes law.

## 6. New Black Democratic cohort prepares to enter Congress

Thirteen new Black Democrats are set to join the House in January, the largest incoming cohort since 1993, while the Senate will include a record seven Black members. A Reuters analysis found that the new lawmakers could influence priorities including voting rights, healthcare affordability and economic inequality, although committee assignments and legislative coalitions will shape their practical power.

**Why it matters:** A generational change in representation may alter Democratic leadership and policy priorities even as the total number of Black House members changes only modestly.

## 7. Xi emerges from summit diplomacy with greater strategic room

Analysts said China's Xi Jinping gained diplomatic standing from hosting the first US–China–Russia summit while offering Donald Trump few concrete concessions on trade, rare earths, detained Americans or Iran. The countries maintained a fragile holding pattern, with another Trump–Xi meeting expected around the December G20 before the current trade truce expires on 10 January.

**Why it matters:** Beijing's ability to delay concessions while sustaining high-level dialogue preserves leverage as technology controls and trade disputes intensify.

## 8. Canada says core military cooperation with United States continues

Canadian Defence Minister David McGuinty said he had detected no change in bilateral military cooperation and had not seen a reported Pentagon memo imposing new constraints. A US senator said the restrictions concerned think-tank events and some social activities rather than joint operations, while McGuinty stressed Canada's need to strengthen Arctic security and sovereign communications.

**Why it matters:** Uncertainty over the memo adds political strain to one of the world's closest defence relationships even as both countries depend on integrated continental and Arctic security.

## 9. Swiss People's Party names two candidates to succeed Parmelin

The Swiss People's Party parliamentary group nominated National Councillors Thomas Aeschi of Zug and Michael Graber of Valais for the Federal Council seat that Guy Parmelin will vacate at year-end. The Federal Assembly is scheduled to choose the replacement on 9 December, with the all-male, German-speaking ticket drawing criticism over regional and gender representation.

**Why it matters:** The choice will shape the composition and internal balance of Switzerland's seven-member government ahead of the 2027 federal election cycle.

## 10. Youth protests spread across Swiss cities

Thousands of young people demonstrated in several Swiss cities against higher military spending, the climate crisis and cuts to education and other public services. The coordinated actions formed part of a wider European youth movement and brought together concerns about rearmament and austerity shortly before Switzerland's winter parliamentary session.

**Why it matters:** The demonstrations show that defence funding is becoming linked to broader generational disputes over public services and climate policy in Swiss politics.
