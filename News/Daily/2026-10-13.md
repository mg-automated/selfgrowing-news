## 1. US diesel proposal exposes divisions over Russia–Ukraine diplomacy

US officials hope a deal under which Russia would supply diesel to American markets could soften Moscow's position in negotiations, but the proposal has drawn bipartisan criticism in Washington. Sources told Reuters that President Vladimir Putin still seeks Ukrainian withdrawal from the remaining Kyiv-held part of Donetsk region, while Ukraine did not agree to a US request to halt refinery strikes even though it paused them temporarily.

**Why it matters:** The disclosed terms show that the latest energy diplomacy has not resolved the central territorial dispute and could give Moscow economic relief without securing a verified ceasefire.

## 2. Saudi warning failures come under scrutiny after Riyadh attacks

Saudi Arabia's public-warning system remained silent before four recent attacks on Riyadh, including strikes on the international airport, Reuters reported. The attacks have killed at least 18 people and wounded more than 300, prompting questions about civil-defence readiness as Houthi strikes reach the capital.

**Why it matters:** The casualty toll and warning-system failure reveal a serious vulnerability in Saudi homeland defence as the Yemen conflict spreads deeper into the Gulf.

## 3. NATO and Germany sharpen warnings over Russian threats

NATO's top operational commander said the alliance would “block, blunt and break” any Russian attack, while stressing that deterrence is intended to prevent such a conflict. Germany's interior minister separately described a Russian “shadow war” of sabotage, disinformation and cyber activity, as Czech intelligence warned that operations against NATO states could become riskier within months.

**Why it matters:** The coordinated warnings signal that European governments are treating hybrid operations and conventional deterrence as parts of the same escalating security challenge.

## 4. China sanctions ministers from Paraguay and Belize over Taiwan ties

China imposed sanctions on Paraguay's communications minister and Belize's foreign minister after they visited Taiwan, barring them from entering China and restricting dealings with Chinese entities. Both countries are among the small number of states that retain formal diplomatic relations with Taipei.

**Why it matters:** Beijing is escalating personal and economic pressure on officials from Taiwan's remaining diplomatic partners in an effort to further isolate the island.

## 5. Federal agents search Representative Ilhan Omar's home

US immigration agents executed a judicially authorised search warrant at Democratic Representative Ilhan Omar's Washington home as part of an investigation reportedly involving alleged immigration fraud. The scope and evidence remain unclear; Omar denied wrongdoing and called the action political intimidation, while senior Democrats questioned its timing three weeks before the midterm elections.

**Why it matters:** A law-enforcement action against a sitting opposition legislator so close to an election raises consequential questions about due process, prosecutorial independence and the use of federal power.

## 6. Swiss coalition launches campaign against looser arms-export rules

A coalition of 28 parties and organisations opened its campaign against revised Swiss arms-export rules, calling them immoral and harmful to Ukraine. Opponents argue that the law would reduce Swiss control over where exported weapons ultimately go, while supporters say easing restrictions would strengthen the domestic defence industry and cooperation with European partners.

**Why it matters:** The dispute puts Switzerland's neutrality, humanitarian policy and defence-industrial ties at the centre of a consequential national political contest.

## 7. Microsoft plans million-chip deployment for its Maia 300 AI accelerator

Microsoft plans to deploy more than one million of its Maia 300 AI chips in 2027 as it seeks to reduce dependence on external suppliers and lower the cost of operating AI services, Reuters reported. The scale would make the company's internally designed accelerator a significant part of its data-centre fleet.

**Why it matters:** Large-scale adoption of custom silicon could reshape cloud-AI economics and intensify competition with Nvidia and other chip suppliers.

## 8. Anthropic says new safeguards sharply reduce model-distillation attacks

Anthropic said new defensive measures cut suspected attempts to extract capabilities from its models through distillation by between 90% and 99%. The company has previously accused overseas groups of using large volumes of model interactions to train competing systems, making access controls and detection an increasingly important part of frontier-model security.

**Why it matters:** Effective anti-distillation controls could protect costly model capabilities, but they also sharpen geopolitical and commercial disputes over access to advanced AI systems.

## 10. IMF says AI investment supports growth while amplifying risks

The IMF said strong AI investment is helping keep global growth near 3%, with several Asian economies benefiting from demand for chips and infrastructure. It also warned that stretched asset valuations, labour disruption, higher bond yields and cyber threats could make the boom a source of financial instability.

**Why it matters:** AI spending is becoming large enough to support the global economy while simultaneously increasing the consequences of a market correction or uneven transition.
