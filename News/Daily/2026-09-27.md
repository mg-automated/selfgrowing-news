# Daily News — 2026-09-27

Coverage window: 2026-09-26T07:44:18+02:00 to 2026-09-27T07:44:18+02:00.

## Overview

Diplomacy produced new contacts but few firm outcomes: Washington rejected Iran's proposal for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, while Russian and German foreign ministers held their first talks since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. China challenged US policy at the United Nations as Taiwan defended continued American arms supplies, and Trump raised possible agreements with Cuba and on Venezuelan elections. In Switzerland, voters prepared to decide neutrality and food-security initiatives, the government pushed back against UBS relocation warnings, and nationwide climate rallies pressed for faster action, while US–China rivalry continued to limit cooperation on artificial intelligence.

## 1. Trump rejects Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

US President Donald Trump said he had rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the wider conflict, arguing that Tehran was seeking an agreement from a position of desperation. Iran continued to insist on a diplomatic solution, but a senior Iranian official maintained that the country would not make concessions over its nuclear programme.

**Why it matters:** The rejection reverses the tentative momentum around a phased shipping agreement and prolongs the risk to energy markets and commercial navigation through a critical waterway.

## 2. Russian and German foreign ministers hold first talks since 2022

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov and German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul met on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, their countries' first foreign-minister-level talks since before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The two sides discussed bilateral relations and the war, while Lavrov separately said Russia was considering proposals involving India, Egypt and Turkey for safer Black Sea navigation.

**Why it matters:** The meeting reopens a direct channel between Moscow and a central European supporter of Ukraine, although it does not by itself signal movement toward a ceasefire.

## 3. China challenges US policy on Iran and Cuba at the United Nations

Chinese Vice President Han Zheng used China's UN General Assembly address to call for respect for Gulf states' sovereignty and to urge the United States to stop threatening Cuba and lift its embargo. He framed consultation and sovereign equality as alternatives to unilateral pressure.

**Why it matters:** The speech shows Beijing using the UN to contest Washington's policies in two active disputes shortly after the Trump–Xi summit failed to resolve major strategic differences.

## 4. Trump predicts a US–Cuba agreement without military action

Trump said he expected the United States and Cuba to reach an agreement and did not believe military action would be necessary. The remarks came as Washington maintained heavy pressure on Havana and China publicly called for an end to US threats and the embargo.

**Why it matters:** Even without a defined negotiating framework, the statement lowers the immediate rhetorical risk of military escalation and introduces a possible diplomatic opening in a long-frozen relationship.

## 6. Taiwan says continued US arms supplies serve American interests

A senior Taiwanese diplomat said US weapons for Taiwan protect both the island's freedom and its role in global semiconductor supply chains. The statement followed the Trump–Xi summit, where Taiwan remained a central unresolved issue and Beijing continued to press Washington to halt arms sales.

**Why it matters:** Taipei is explicitly tying its security to US economic interests as it seeks reassurance that great-power diplomacy will not weaken its defensive support.

## 7. Switzerland prepares to vote on neutrality and food security

Swiss voters were set to decide whether to write a stricter definition of neutrality into the constitution and whether to require greater food self-sufficiency and support for plant-based production. Both initiatives need a majority of voters and cantons, and pre-vote polling indicated that majorities opposed them.

**Why it matters:** The neutrality proposal could sharply constrain Switzerland's ability to impose sanctions independently, while the food initiative would redirect agricultural and environmental policy.

## 8. Swiss finance minister dismisses UBS relocation warnings

Finance Minister Karin Keller-Sutter said moving UBS abroad to avoid stricter capital requirements would be more expensive and legally complex than remaining in Switzerland. Swiss National Bank President Martin Schlegel also stressed that Switzerland brings substantial benefits to UBS, after the bank renewed discussion of possible relocation options.

**Why it matters:** The comments harden the government's response to UBS lobbying and signal that policymakers are unlikely to retreat simply because the bank raises the possibility of leaving.

## 9. Thousands join climate demonstrations across Switzerland

Several thousand people demonstrated in Swiss cities after a record-hot summer, calling for faster measures against heatwaves and climate change. Organisers said roughly 4,000 people attended in Basel, with further rallies in Bern, Zurich, Lucerne, Bellinzona and other cities.

**Why it matters:** The nationwide mobilisation increases public pressure for adaptation and emissions policy after extreme heat made the economic and social costs of climate change more tangible.

## 10. US and China establish an AI safety channel but rule out joint development

The United States and China agreed to establish a channel for handling artificial-intelligence incidents and to continue work on military crisis communications. Trump nevertheless ruled out combining US and Chinese efforts to develop the technology, while leaving open continued discussion with Beijing.

**Why it matters:** A dedicated safety channel could reduce misunderstanding around dangerous AI incidents, but the refusal to cooperate on development confirms that the broader technology race remains intensely competitive.
