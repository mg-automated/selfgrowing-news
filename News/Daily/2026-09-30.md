## 1. Russia widens its seizure of European companies' assets

Russia placed the local assets of Nestlé, Metro and Auchan under temporary administration as part of a widening campaign against European businesses. Russian government and corporate sources described the measures as retaliation for European military support for Ukraine and sanctions, while signalling that other European companies could also be targeted.

**Why it matters:** The campaign raises the cost of remaining in Russia for European companies and turns private assets into leverage in the broader confrontation between Moscow and Europe.

## 2. Myanmar airstrike reportedly kills 50 people in Rakhine State

The United Nations said it was alarmed by reports that a Myanmar military airstrike on Kyauktaw in Rakhine State killed 50 people and wounded at least 58. The Arakan Army, which controls the area, and a local aid worker reported the toll; the UN said the attack showed that conditions did not permit the safe return of displaced people and refugees.

**Why it matters:** The strike is among the deadliest recent attacks in Rakhine and underlines the acute danger facing civilians near the Bangladesh border.

**Topics:** [Myanmar](../../Topics/Myanmar.md), [United Nations](../../Topics/United-Nations.md)

## 3. Russian strikes on Kyiv kill three, including a child

Russian airstrikes killed three people, including a child, and injured seven in Kyiv and the surrounding region early Wednesday, Ukrainian officials said. The attack followed weeks of intensified ballistic-missile and jet-powered-drone operations that have strained Ukraine's air defences and repeatedly disrupted civilian life.

**Why it matters:** The deaths mark a fresh escalation in the sustained air campaign and highlight Ukraine's continuing shortage of high-end interceptors.

**Topics:** [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md)

## 4. Pakistan pledges to defend Saudi Arabia against the Houthis

Pakistan's defence minister said Islamabad would use "whatever means are available" to defend Saudi Arabia against the Houthis under a new mutual-defence pact with Saudi Arabia and Turkey. He did not say whether Pakistani forces had already participated in strikes or whether Riyadh had formally requested assistance.

**Why it matters:** The statement creates a clearer path for Pakistan to become directly involved in a regional conflict already threatening Saudi territory and Red Sea shipping.

## 5. UN envoy meets Houthi negotiators as Yemen violence escalates

UN envoy Hans Grundberg met Houthi negotiators in Muscat and officials in Saudi Arabia in an effort to prevent further escalation in Yemen. The diplomatic push follows Houthi territorial gains, renewed attacks on Saudi Arabia and mounting disruption around the Bab el-Mandeb shipping route.

**Why it matters:** A renewed negotiating channel could help contain a conflict that is increasingly intertwined with the wider regional war and global maritime trade.

## 6. Swiss Senate advances EU treaties and adds an immigration levy

The Council of States voted 29–15 to proceed with the Bilaterals III treaty package and then approved an incentive levy as an additional safeguard if immigration causes serious economic or social difficulties. The levy goes beyond the Federal Council's proposed safeguards and drew opposition from the left and some centre-right senators from French-speaking Switzerland.

**Why it matters:** The decisions move the treaty package forward while adding a measure that could complicate the domestic coalition behind it and Switzerland's negotiations with the European Union.

## 7. Swiss government campaigns to relax arms-export rules

The Federal Council launched its campaign for a yes vote in the November 29 referendum on revised war-materiel rules. The government argues that allowing greater flexibility for exports and re-exports under specified conditions is necessary to preserve Switzerland's defence-industrial capacity and employment.

**Why it matters:** The vote will test how Switzerland balances neutrality and control over weapons transfers against defence cooperation and the viability of its arms industry.

## 8. Swiss health-insurance premiums to rise 5% in 2027

Switzerland's Federal Office of Public Health said mandatory health-insurance premiums will rise by an average of 5% in 2027. The average monthly premium will reach CHF412, an increase of CHF19.70, with the average adult premium rising to CHF487.60.

**Why it matters:** Another above-inflation increase will put additional pressure on household budgets and intensify political debate over healthcare costs and premium relief.

## 9. Trump and leading AI companies sign voluntary safety accord

President Donald Trump and executives from major AI companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Google and Nvidia, signed a voluntary accord providing for internal controls and external reviews of their systems. Trump described the agreement as morally binding and suggested that an oversight committee could monitor implementation, but the commitments are not statutory regulation.

**Why it matters:** The accord establishes a shared safety baseline across leading developers while leaving enforcement dependent largely on corporate cooperation and political oversight.

## 10. Visa open-sources part of its AI cyber-defence system

Visa open-sourced part of its AI-based cyber-defence system as it warned that autonomous models could make attacks faster, more adaptive and less dependent on human operators. The payments company said defensive systems will increasingly need comparable automation as AI agents take on a larger role in commerce.

**Why it matters:** Opening defensive technology to wider use could strengthen collective resilience while signalling that major financial networks expect agentic cyber threats to become a systemic risk.
