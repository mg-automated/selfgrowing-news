## 1. United States disables a ship trying to leave Iran

US Central Command said military aircraft struck the stern of the Palau-flagged *Pacific Archer* after it departed Bandar Abbas, disabling the commercial ship's propulsion without harming its civilian crew. An adviser to Iran's supreme leader said Tehran would keep the Strait of Hormuz closed and break the US blockade, while Iran's navy said it was continuing maritime escorts.

**Why it matters:** The direct use of force against commercial shipping and Iran's vow to resist the blockade increase the risk of further military escalation around a waterway central to global energy trade.

## 2. Russian military convoy turns away from Libya after warship sinking

A convoy of Russian cargo ships carrying military vehicles turned around in the Mediterranean after Ukrainian maritime drones sank the Russian corvette *Merkury* in Benghazi on November 22, three military and security sources told Reuters. The vessels may have been carrying supplies for Russia's Africa Corps or forces aligned with eastern Libyan commander Khalifa Haftar; Russia's defence ministry did not comment.

**Why it matters:** The diversion shows that Ukrainian strikes far from the main battlefield are affecting Russia's ability to sustain military relationships and operations in Africa.

**Topics:** [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md), [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md)

## 3. Islamic State claims an attack that killed 13 soldiers in Niger

Niger's defence ministry said 13 National Guard soldiers were killed and 20 wounded when militants attacked a position in the country's southwest. Islamic State claimed responsibility for the assault through its Telegram channel.

**Why it matters:** The losses underscore the persistent reach of jihadist groups in the Sahel despite military-led governments' promises to restore security.

**Topics:** [Niger](../../Topics/Niger.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/suspected-militants-kill-13-soldiers-southwest-niger-ministry-says-2026-11-27/)

## 4. Canada's top court upholds Quebec's religious-symbol ban

Canada's Supreme Court upheld Quebec's law barring some public servants from wearing overt religious symbols, confirming that the province could shield the measure from most rights challenges through the Constitution's notwithstanding clause. The court struck down only a provision affecting legislators who must cover their faces for religious reasons, while rights groups warned that the wider law disproportionately harms Muslim women.

**Why it matters:** The ruling strengthens provincial power to override protected rights and may intensify debates over minority protections, secularism and Canadian unity.

**Topics:** [Canada](../../Topics/Canada.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/canadas-supreme-court-rule-quebecs-law-religious-symbols-test-canadian-unity-2026-11-27/)

## 5. Taiwan closes local-election campaign amid Chinese military pressure

Taiwan's parties held their final rallies before voting in all 22 cities and counties, with the Taipei mayoral contest drawing particular attention before the 2028 presidential race. Taiwan's defence ministry also detected 30 Chinese military aircraft around the island, including 26 that crossed the Taiwan Strait's median line during a joint combat-readiness patrol with warships.

**Why it matters:** Although the elections focus mainly on local issues, their results will shape national political momentum while Beijing continues military and political pressure on the island.

**Topics:** [Taiwan](../../Topics/Taiwan.md), [Pacific Security](../../Topics/Pacific-Security.md), [China](../../Topics/China.md)

## 6. Dutch prime minister plans to challenge proposed US chip-control law

Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten said he would raise the proposed MATCH Act with US President Donald Trump at the December G20 summit. The legislation could restrict US technology exports to allied countries that do not adopt controls comparable to Washington's limits on chipmaking equipment for China, a concern for the Netherlands and its leading equipment maker ASML.

**Why it matters:** The dispute tests whether Washington can extend its China technology policy through allied supply chains without provoking European retaliation or weakening transatlantic cooperation.

## 7. SAP prepares sizeable job cuts as artificial intelligence reshapes software

German business-software group SAP is preparing a large reduction in its workforce as it reorganises around artificial intelligence, sources told Reuters. Bloomberg reported that at least 10,000 positions could be affected, but SAP did not confirm a number and its works council said it had not been formally informed.

**Why it matters:** Potential cuts at Germany's most valuable company illustrate how AI investment is beginning to alter employment even at profitable, established technology firms.

**Topics:** [Artificial Intelligence](../../Topics/Artificial-Intelligence.md), [Germany](../../Topics/Germany.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/sap-seeks-cut-sizeable-number-jobs-sources-say-2026-11-27/)

## 8. Funding cuts close nearly half of surveyed shelters for displaced women

The UN refugee agency said 171 of 374 safe spaces for displaced women and girls across 14 countries closed in 2025, with further closures this year as gender-based-violence programmes received less than a quarter of requested 2026 funding. The facilities provide protection and access to psychological and other support for survivors of violence in conflicts including Sudan, Yemen and the Democratic Republic of Congo.

**Why it matters:** The closures remove essential protection precisely where displacement and crowded camps expose women and girls to greater risks of sexual violence and abuse.

**Topics:** [United Nations](../../Topics/United-Nations.md), [Sudan](../../Topics/Sudan.md), [Yemen Conflict](../../Topics/Yemen-Conflict.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/funding-cuts-force-mass-closures-shelters-refugee-women-un-says-2026-11-27/)

## 9. Global measles deaths fall to a historic low while vaccination gaps persist

The World Health Organization estimated that measles deaths fell to about 72,000 in 2025, roughly 91% below their 2000 level, while cases declined to 9.8 million. It also said 29 million children remained unvaccinated or under-vaccinated and 59 countries experienced large or disruptive outbreaks.

**Why it matters:** The long-term decline demonstrates the impact of vaccination, but persistent coverage gaps and pressure on health budgets leave room for rapid and preventable resurgences.

**Topics:** [Global Health](../../Topics/Global-Health.md), [United Nations](../../Topics/United-Nations.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/global-measles-deaths-fall-historic-low-2025-who-says-2026-11-27/)

## 10. Swiss Federal Court rejects appeal over Basel e-voting failure

The Federal Court declared inadmissible an appeal by a Swiss voter abroad whose electronic ballot was among 2,048 votes that could not be counted in Basel during the March 8 federal referendums. It ruled that Basel's cantonal government had already fully addressed her complaint by recognising that the technical failure infringed her voting rights; she had not challenged the referendum result itself.

**Why it matters:** The judgment closes the individual case without resolving broader questions about operational responsibility and public confidence in Switzerland's suspended e-voting trials.
