## Overview

The day combined unsuccessful great-power diplomacy with another sharp escalation in the Russia–Ukraine war: the Trump–Xi summit ended without a major breakthrough, Washington publicly pressed Beijing over support for Iran, and both Russia and Ukraine launched large drone attacks. Iran separately ruled out nuclear concessions, while Switzerland announced a new cybersecurity law, a housing-policy counterproposal, increased integration funding and Zurich as host of the next European Political Community summit. ## 1. Trump–Xi summit ends without a major breakthrough

US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping concluded their Washington summit without a major agreement on trade, Taiwan, Iran or the countries' intensifying technology competition. The meeting nevertheless kept senior-level dialogue open and included discussion of military communications and human oversight of artificial intelligence.

**Why it matters:** The absence of a rupture reduces near-term escalation risk, but leaving the central disputes unresolved preserves uncertainty for global trade, semiconductor supply chains and security in the Taiwan Strait.

## 2. Trump tells Xi that Chinese support for Iran is unacceptable

Trump told Xi during their White House talks that Chinese assistance to Iran was unacceptable, US Ambassador to China David Perdue said. The intervention tied the bilateral summit directly to Washington's effort to pressure Tehran over the war and the Strait of Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** Chinese economic ties give Beijing potential leverage over Tehran, but US demands that it use that leverage could turn the Iran conflict into another point of confrontation between the world's two largest economies.

## 3. Russian attacks on Kyiv kill seven as strikes continue into daytime

Russian attacks on Kyiv killed at least seven people, including two children, and injured dozens, according to Ukrainian authorities. The updated toll and continuing daytime strikes marked a substantial escalation from the initial residential-building attack reported a day earlier.

**Why it matters:** The mounting civilian toll shows that diplomatic discussion has not reduced the immediate threat to Ukraine's cities and is likely to intensify demands for additional air-defence support.

## 4. Ukraine launches a record-scale drone barrage against Russian targets

Russia's Defence Ministry said it intercepted 592 Ukrainian drones overnight over Russian regions, occupied Crimea and the Black Sea. Reports indicated that oil-refining and industrial facilities were among the targets, extending Ukraine's campaign against infrastructure that supports Russia's war economy.

**Why it matters:** The scale of the attack demonstrates Ukraine's expanding long-range capacity and raises the costs of protecting Russian energy and industrial assets far from the front.

## 5. Iran rules out nuclear concessions in Strait negotiations

A senior Iranian official said Tehran would make no concessions on its nuclear programme even if Washington accepted Iran's proposal for reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The proposal links renewed shipping access to measures including the lifting of the US naval blockade on Iranian ports.

**Why it matters:** Iran's position narrows the scope of a possible phased deal and separates the immediate shipping crisis from the harder nuclear dispute at the centre of the conflict.

## 6. Switzerland plans a standalone Cybersecurity Act

The Federal Council instructed the defence department to prepare a consultation draft for a new Federal Act on Cybersecurity by June 2027. Modelled partly on the EU Cyber Resilience Act, the bill is intended to consolidate requirements for digital products, important data, hosting and cloud providers, and the reporting of attacks on critical infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** A single framework would extend binding security responsibilities across Switzerland's digital supply chain while keeping national rules aligned with the European market.

**Sources:** [Swiss Federal Office for Cybersecurity](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26-cybersicherheitsgesetz-en)

## 7. Federal Council counters rent-control initiative with narrower reform

The Federal Council rejected the popular initiative calling for automatic and regular rent controls, but said it would submit an indirect counterproposal. The government said the alternative would establish clearer and more transparent rules for setting rents and protecting tenants from abusive increases.

**Why it matters:** The counterproposal opens a legislative route to reform Switzerland's politically sensitive rental market without adopting the initiative's broader control mechanism.

## 8. Switzerland increases federal funding for integration programmes

The Federal Council approved a CHF334.5 million commitment for federal and cantonal integration measures from 2028 through 2032. Planned annual federal spending rises to CHF66.9 million, supporting language instruction, information and counselling, education and labour-market integration.

**Why it matters:** The additional funding expands the long-term capacity of cantons to integrate a growing eligible population and links migration policy more closely to education and employment outcomes.

**Sources:** [Swiss Federal Council](https://www.wbf.admin.ch/de/newnsb/DU299Gbe1zOk)

## 9. Zurich chosen to host the next European Political Community summit

Switzerland will host the tenth European Political Community summit at The Circle in Zurich in April 2027, the Federal Council decided. Around 50 European heads of state and government, together with international organisations, are expected to attend.

**Why it matters:** Hosting the forum gives non-EU Switzerland a prominent platform for security and diplomatic coordination across the continent at a time of sustained geopolitical strain.

## 10. US Senate returns to address data-centre electricity costs

US Senate Republicans decided to return before the midterm elections to take up the Ratepayer Protection Act, which seeks to shield households from grid costs linked to rapidly expanding data centres. The move reflects mounting voter concern over electricity prices and the infrastructure demands of the AI build-out.

**Why it matters:** Data-centre power demand is becoming an electoral issue, increasing the prospect that AI infrastructure developers will face new requirements to pay more of the cost of grid expansion.
