# Daily News — 2026-09-22

Coverage window: 2026-09-21T09:00:32+02:00 to 2026-09-22T09:00:32+02:00.

## Overview

Political and security developments dominated the day, from a sweeping purge at the top of China's military and new pressure over Taiwan arms sales to a broad Ethiopian opposition alliance, a North Korean missile test and a G7 demand that Iran stop supporting the Houthis. In Switzerland, lawmakers pressed the government over the troubled F-35 procurement while the Senate rejected tighter rules for paid signature collection. Technology policy and strategic competition converged as Alibaba expanded its AI-chip ambitions, OpenAI proposed international frontier-model standards, and Britain and the United States prepared deeper cooperation on military AI.

## 1. China expels two of its most senior generals

China expelled Central Military Commission vice-chair Zhang Youxia and Joint Staff chief Liu Zhenli from the Communist Party and the armed forces after investigations accused them of serious disloyalty, faction-building and corruption. Their removal leaves only President Xi Jinping and one other active member on a commission that previously had seven, while the cases are being transferred to prosecutors.

**Why it matters:** The purge removes two of China's most powerful uniformed officers and further concentrates control over the military at a time of heightened regional tension.

## 2. Ethiopian opposition groups form a coalition against Abiy's government

Seven Ethiopian opposition organisations announced the Ethiopian Peoples' Forces Alliance for Survival, bringing together groups including the Tigray People's Liberation Front, the Oromo Liberation Army and Amhara Fano forces. The alliance said it would pursue political and military cooperation to remove Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government, and a TPLF spokesman said joint military operations could become necessary.

**Why it matters:** Cooperation among previously separate armed and political opponents raises the risk of a wider conflict in a country still recovering from the Tigray war.

## 3. G7 foreign ministers call on Iran to stop arming the Houthis

G7 foreign ministers called on Iran to end weapons transfers and other support to the Houthis and condemned the group's attacks in Yemen, Saudi Arabia and against civilian shipping. They also urged the Houthis to halt military action and return to a political process.

**Why it matters:** The joint statement increases coordinated diplomatic pressure on Tehran and the Houthis as attacks continue to threaten regional stability and maritime trade.

## 4. Xi expected to press Trump to halt US arms sales to Taiwan

Chinese President Xi Jinping is expected to ask US President Donald Trump to halt arms sales to Taiwan under Beijing's interpretation of a 1982 bilateral communiqué when the leaders meet in Washington, Reuters reported. The issue comes as Trump considers a new weapons package worth about $14 billion after approving roughly $11 billion in sales, while China has intensified military activity around Taiwan.

**Why it matters:** Taiwan remains the most dangerous point of friction in US-China relations, and any shift in American arms policy would affect deterrence across the Taiwan Strait.

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-xi-expected-press-trump-halt-taiwan-arms-sales-under-1982-agreement-2026-09-22/)

## 5. North Korea tests a new short-range weapon system

North Korea said it tested a new combat weapon system under leader Kim Jong Un's supervision, while South Korea's military reported detecting two short-range ballistic missiles. Images released by state media appeared to show an upgraded missile with a manoeuvrable, potentially hypersonic glide vehicle, according to analysts cited by Reuters.

**Why it matters:** The test suggests continued progress in systems designed to evade regional missile defences and adds pressure on security coordination among South Korea, Japan and the United States.

**Topics:** [North Korea](../../Topics/North-Korea.md), [Pacific Security](../../Topics/Pacific-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/north-korea-says-it-tests-new-combat-weapon-system-kcna-reports-2026-09-21/)

## 6. Swiss lawmakers grill defence minister over the F-35 procurement

Swiss Defence Minister Martin Pfister faced 19 parliamentary questions following an auditors' report that found serious shortcomings in negotiations for the F-35 fighter purchase and concluded that no fixed price had been secured despite earlier government statements. Switzerland has reduced the planned order from 36 to 30 aircraft and requested an additional CHF394 million, with the Federal Council due to respond formally to the report in December.

**Why it matters:** The dispute tests parliamentary oversight of one of Switzerland's largest defence procurements and could influence confidence in future military spending.

## 7. Swiss Senate rejects a ban on paid signature collection

The Council of States rejected proposals to prohibit paid collection of signatures for popular initiatives and referendums, and it also declined to require collectors to disclose who financed their work. The proposals followed allegations of illegal practices in commercial signature gathering that prompted criminal complaints in 2024.

**Why it matters:** The decision preserves a contested campaign practice at the heart of Switzerland's direct-democracy system despite concerns about transparency and fraud.

**Topics:** [Swiss Direct Democracy](../../Topics/Swiss-Direct-Democracy.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-democracy/collecting-signatures-in-exchange-for-money-remains-permitted/92094669)

## 8. Alibaba unveils a faster AI chip and plans much larger models

Alibaba said it is developing future Qwen models with five trillion to ten trillion parameters, compared with 2.4 trillion in its current largest model, and unveiled the Zhenwu V900 AI chip. The company said the chip delivers three times the performance of its predecessor, can be deployed in clusters of up to 500,000 units and is expected to enter mass production for commercial use in the first quarter of 2027.

**Why it matters:** The announcement shows Chinese technology companies accelerating domestic AI hardware and model development as access to leading foreign chips remains constrained.

## 9. OpenAI calls for US-led international standards for frontier AI

OpenAI urged the United States to lead international work on technical standards for advanced AI, including common measurements for capabilities such as recursive self-improvement and shared incident-reporting practices. The company argued that coordinated standards could help safety and alignment measures keep pace as frontier systems become more capable.

**Why it matters:** Common tests and reporting rules could shape how governments evaluate and govern the most advanced models before incompatible national regimes become entrenched.

## 10. Britain and the United States deepen cooperation on military AI

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham prepared to announce a defence AI partnership linking the UK's Defence Rapid AI Delivery Taskforce with the US Department of War's Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office. The initiative is intended to develop trusted, interoperable AI capabilities for defence, critical infrastructure and deterrence.

**Why it matters:** Closer operational cooperation could accelerate military AI deployment while making shared standards and safeguards more consequential for both countries.
