## 1. COP31 ends without an overarching climate agreement

The COP31 summit in Antalya ended without a political cover decision after the European Union and other governments rejected a draft that omitted any reference to fossil fuels. Negotiators also remained divided over carbon capture, climate finance and the pace of the clean-energy transition, marking the first COP collapse of this kind since 2009.

**Why it matters:** Failure to agree a common political direction weakens the UN climate process at a time when emissions cuts and financing commitments are already falling short.

## 2. Suspected drone attack sinks Russian warship in Benghazi

Libya's eastern-based government said a suspected drone attack caused an explosion aboard a Russian vessel at the Benghazi naval base, with satellite imagery identifying it as the corvette Merkury and showing most of it submerged. Ten Russians and Libyans were slightly injured, while a Maltese-flagged commercial vessel was later hit offshore and Libyan forces said they intercepted more than 20 drone boats; no group claimed responsibility.

**Why it matters:** The attacks risk extending maritime warfare involving Russian assets into the Mediterranean and turning divided Libya into another arena for external conflict.

## 3. Drone-struck Russian cargo ship is towed from Finnish waters

Russia began towing the Sparta II after drones struck the sanctioned cargo vessel in international waters and it drifted into Finnish waters while burning. Finland said Ukraine was probably responsible, all 16 crew members were evacuated, and Finnish and Estonian services helped contain the fire as authorities warned that further Gulf of Finland attacks could not be ruled out.

**Why it matters:** An attack close to NATO waters raises escalation and maritime-safety risks around the Baltic route to Russia's Kaliningrad exclave.

## 4. Settler arson attacks deepen Israel's political and security crisis

Suspected militant Israeli settlers set fire to vehicles and property in Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank and painted racist graffiti. Gadi Eisenkot, who is trying to form Israel's next government, called the violence “Jewish terrorism” and blamed the outgoing right-wing government for failing to contain it.

**Why it matters:** Escalating settler violence threatens Palestinian civilians, regional stability and Israel's coalition negotiations while exposing a central divide over enforcement in the West Bank.

## 5. Drone strikes damage UN compound in Khartoum

Drones struck Khartoum early Sunday, damaging a UN facility in a residential neighbourhood while staff were inside. The United Nations condemned the attack and said the increasing use of drones across Sudan was killing civilians and putting protected humanitarian personnel and infrastructure at risk.

**Why it matters:** Damage to a UN compound shows how Sudan's drone war is reaching civilian and humanitarian sites even after residents began returning to the capital.

## 6. Fighting disrupts response to Congo's uncontrolled Ebola outbreak

World Health Organization chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said heavy fighting near a hospital in Ituri forced health workers and aid partners to shelter in a maternity ward for more than 24 hours. The outbreak remains uncontrolled six months after it was declared, with government figures recording more than 11,000 infections and 5,500 deaths.

**Why it matters:** Insecurity is obstructing containment of a rapidly growing epidemic and placing patients, health workers and neighbouring communities at greater risk.

## 7. Democrats secure their largest US House majority since 1992

Democrats reached at least 258 House seats after Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina, with four races still unresolved. The result gives the party a wide governing margin when it takes control in January alongside a 56-seat Senate caucus, while a Republican remains in the White House.

**Why it matters:** The scale of the majority gives Democrats more room to pass legislation, conduct oversight and absorb internal defections than recent narrowly divided Congresses allowed.

## 8. US government demands answers from Nvidia over China chip sales

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said US officials had contacted Nvidia's interim management, board and lawyers and wanted to know who had responsibility for compliance around chip sales to China. The scrutiny follows Jensen Huang's resignation, multiple investigations and tighter US controls on Chinese access to advanced AI processors.

**Why it matters:** The inquiry could reshape governance at the world's leading AI-chip supplier and influence how aggressively Washington enforces technology restrictions on China.

## 9. US intelligence chief warns that technology outages will continue

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said companies and government agencies should expect further service interruptions as they replace ageing systems under pressure from new technology and cybersecurity demands. He attributed this month's aviation, banking, Microsoft and National Security Agency disruptions to upgrade problems rather than a coordinated state-sponsored campaign.

**Why it matters:** Repeated failures across critical services expose the operational risk of modernising interconnected legacy systems even when hostile activity is not responsible.

## 10. Swiss media workers rally against newsroom cuts

Nearly 2,000 people gathered on Parliament Square in Bern to protest job losses and the weakening of Swiss journalism, according to organisers. The Media Alliance said more than 360 positions had been eliminated or marked for redundancy this year and argued that shrinking newsrooms threaten the media's democratic role across all four language regions.

**Why it matters:** Consolidation and public-service cuts are turning the economic crisis in Swiss media into a broader debate about press freedom, regional coverage and democratic accountability.
