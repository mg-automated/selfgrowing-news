## 1. Swiss voters reject stricter neutrality and food-security initiatives

Swiss voters rejected the Neutrality Initiative by 70.2%, with every canton voting against the proposed stricter constitutional definition, while turnout reached 47%. The Food Initiative, which sought to raise domestic food self-sufficiency and encourage plant-based production, was defeated by 72.5%.

**Why it matters:** The double rejection preserves the government's flexibility to align with sanctions and security partners while leaving agricultural and food policy to the existing political process.

## 2. Serbia's president resigns to seek the premiership

Serbian President Aleksandar Vučić resigned seven months before the end of his term, allowing him to run for prime minister in the snap parliamentary election scheduled for October 25. The move comes after nearly two years of student-led protests over corruption, transparency and political interference in state institutions.

**Why it matters:** Vučić is attempting to preserve executive power through a different office while Serbia faces its most sustained domestic challenge to his rule in more than a decade.

## 3. Five arrested near British air base used for US strikes on Iran

British counter-terrorism police arrested five men near RAF Fairford on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act after reports of vans heading toward the base. Army specialists examined vehicles and nearby properties were evacuated; the base has been used by US forces for strikes on Iranian missile sites.

**Why it matters:** The incident shows how the Iran conflict is creating security risks beyond the Middle East and forcing greater protection of military infrastructure in Europe.

## 8. Foreign banks reportedly express interest in combining with UBS

At least eight large foreign banks have signalled interest in a possible merger or combination with UBS, Swiss newspaper *Blick* reported, citing an informed source. The report followed the Swiss upper house's support for tougher capital rules and UBS warnings that the requirements could affect its future in Switzerland.

**Why it matters:** Concrete external interest raises the stakes in the regulatory dispute by turning UBS's relocation warnings into potential strategic options, even though no transaction has been confirmed.

**Why it matters:** Approval would reopen an important market for Nvidia and give Chinese AI developers additional computing capacity while domestic suppliers work to meet demand.
