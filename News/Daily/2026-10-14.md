## Overview

Military and political pressure intensified as Russia struck Kyiv's energy infrastructure despite ceasefire claims, Iran widened its campaign against shipping, and Ethiopian forces reached the Eritrean border amid fears of a regional war. ## 1. Russian missiles strike Kyiv energy infrastructure despite ceasefire claims

Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv, killing two people in an apartment building, injuring 21 and striking a thermal power plant. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine received no advance warning from Western partners for the second attack in 24 hours and argued that the strikes showed Moscow did not consider itself bound by the energy ceasefire announced by US President Donald Trump.

**Why it matters:** The attack undermines confidence in the proposed ceasefire while exposing Ukraine's continuing dependence on allied intelligence for civilian warning and air defence.

## 2. Iran expands attacks on shipping around the Strait of Hormuz

Senior Iranian officials told Reuters that Tehran had changed the pace, methods and geographical reach of attacks on vessels, including the use of unarmed small-boat spotters after losing radar capabilities. Maritime data recorded at least five incidents involving commercial ships within 48 hours around Hormuz, while officials said operations could extend farther into the Gulf of Oman and nearby waters.

**Why it matters:** A deliberate expansion of attacks increases risks to a route that normally carries about one-fifth of global oil and liquefied-natural-gas trade and could amplify energy-market and military escalation.

## 3. Ethiopian forces reach Eritrean border as regional-war fears grow

Ethiopian government troops advanced to Zalambessa on the Eritrean border after driving a Tigrayan-led rebel alliance north, according to witnesses, a diplomat and a conflict monitor. Ethiopia accused Eritrean forces of crossing as far as 60 kilometres into its territory, which Eritrea denied, while the UN Security Council prepared to discuss the crisis behind closed doors.

**Why it matters:** Renewed direct confrontation between Ethiopia and Eritrea could transform the revived Tigray conflict into a wider Horn of Africa war.

## 4. EU extends migration cooperation to eastern Libya

The European Union allocated an additional €25 million for border management, search-and-rescue and anti-smuggling operations in eastern Libya, according to a letter from European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. The move deepens engagement with territory controlled by military commander Khalifa Haftar even though the EU formally recognises only Libya's Tripoli-based government.

**Why it matters:** The funding expands Europe's reliance on fragmented Libyan authorities to limit Mediterranean crossings, raising strategic and humanitarian questions about accountability.

**Topics:** [European Migration](../../Topics/European-Migration.md), [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/eu-expands-migration-cooperation-with-eastern-libya-through-new-funding-2026-10-13/)

## 5. Berlin coalition negotiations are suspended over antisemitism safeguards

Berlin's Social Democrats and Greens suspended exploratory coalition talks with the Left Party while seeking clarification that previously agreed antisemitism measures would be implemented. The dispute followed the Left's admission of lawmaker Vanessa Emde to its parliamentary group; the Left, which won the September state election with 25.7%, said all its representatives had signed a declaration opposing antisemitism.

**Why it matters:** The dispute threatens formation of Berlin's next government and shows how disagreements over Israel and antisemitism are reshaping coalition politics in Germany.

**Topics:** [Germany](../../Topics/Germany.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/berlin-coalition-talks-called-off-over-antisemitism-deal-concerns-2026-10-13/)

## 6. Swiss government campaigns against married-couples tax initiative

The Federal Council launched its campaign against the Centre Party's constitutional initiative on taxation of married couples ahead of the November 29 vote. The government argues that voters already approved individual taxation in March and that introducing the initiative's alternative calculation method could create conflicting systems, favour married couples and reduce federal revenue by as much as CHF3 billion annually.

**Why it matters:** The vote will determine whether Switzerland stays with its newly approved individual-taxation model or reopens a fundamental dispute over marital status and tax equality.

## 7. Swiss alliance starts referendum challenge against Mercosur trade deal

A Swiss alliance began collecting signatures for a referendum against the EFTA–Mercosur free-trade agreement signed on September 16. The group argues that the accord would encourage imports linked to rainforest loss, intensive agriculture, community displacement and forced labour while creating risks for Swiss farmers; it must submit 50,000 valid signatures by January 21, 2027.

**Why it matters:** A successful referendum would put Switzerland's trade strategy, agricultural protections and environmental standards before voters.

**Topics:** [Swiss Direct Democracy](../../Topics/Swiss-Direct-Democracy.md), [Swiss Climate Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Climate-Policy.md)

## 8. Anthropic warns smaller AI models may approach autonomous replication

Anthropic said experiments showed models with 30 billion parameters or fewer could already perform many individual tasks needed to copy themselves across networks, although they could not yet complete the full process reliably. The company projected that continued capability gains could enable autonomous replication by the end of 2026, while outside experts described the scenario as worth studying but disputed how likely or imminent it is.

**Why it matters:** Models capable of spreading without a central service could create new botnet and cyberattack risks that existing shutdown and containment methods are not designed to handle.

## 9. AI laboratories and evaluators negotiate limits on independent reporting

Anthropic and OpenAI have been negotiating publication rights, confidentiality safeguards and access conditions for independent model evaluations, Reuters reported. Both companies say evaluators should control methods and findings, but discussions over redactions, observation by laboratory staff and restrictions on simultaneous work for rivals show unresolved tension between transparency and trade-secret protection.

**Why it matters:** Without enforceable standards, frontier-model developers may retain substantial influence over the evidence available to regulators, researchers and the public about AI risks.

## 10. Scaleway plans Europe's first commercial AMD AI supercomputer

French cloud provider Scaleway said it would build its first AI supercomputer outside France in Sweden using AMD Instinct MI355X accelerators and the open-source ROCm software ecosystem. The commercially accessible system will be assembled and maintained by Bull and is intended for startups, AI companies and other European businesses.

**Why it matters:** The project broadens European access to large-scale computing while offering an alternative to Nvidia's dominant proprietary hardware and software stack.
