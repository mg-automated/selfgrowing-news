**Why it matters:** Security allegations and surveillance concerns risk further eroding trust in an election already marked by intense political confrontation.

## 4. Critical PHP flaws force government websites offline

Government, hospital and school websites across Europe were taken offline after researchers disclosed two critical vulnerabilities in PHP, which powers much of the public web. AI systems helped researchers uncover the flaws, but security experts warned that the same tools could rapidly reverse-engineer patches and accelerate exploitation of unprotected servers.

**Why it matters:** The incident exposes a systemic vulnerability in widely deployed open-source infrastructure and shows how AI compresses both defensive discovery and offensive exploitation timelines.

## 5. Chinese-linked hackers compromise ConnectWise in global campaign

Microsoft said a Chinese state-aligned group compromised ConnectWise Cloud Backup and targeted hundreds of organisations using an AI-driven penetration-testing framework. ConnectWise contained affected connections, but warned that attackers may have retained privileged access inside individual customer environments; China denied involvement.

**Why it matters:** Compromising a widely used IT provider can give attackers scalable access across sensitive industries and illustrates the growing operational role of autonomous cyber tools.

## 10. Swiss government proposes carbon levy on imported cement

The Federal Council proposed a CO₂ compensation levy on cement imports above a specified quantity to align their carbon costs with Swiss production. The measure is intended to prevent domestic producers facing climate rules from being undercut by higher-emission imports.

**Why it matters:** A border carbon measure would extend Swiss climate policy into trade and could affect construction costs and relations with cement-exporting countries.
