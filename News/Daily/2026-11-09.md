## 1. European governments secure a role in Abu Dhabi Ukraine talks

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said European countries, including France, will participate in the November 10–11 Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi. The US-organised meetings will bring together Russian and Ukrainian delegations to discuss ending the war, post-war security guarantees and de-escalation, while European representation will focus on the wider security architecture.

**Why it matters:** European participation gives governments most directly affected by a settlement a place in negotiations that had risked being shaped primarily by Washington and Moscow.

## 2. Trump says progress in talks makes new Iran strikes unlikely

President Donald Trump said fresh US attacks on Iran were “highly unlikely” if negotiations to end the conflict made progress, while warning that Washington had identified infrastructure targets if diplomacy failed. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said a breakthrough was not imminent, and shipping incidents around the Strait of Hormuz continued as Qatar mediated between the sides.

**Why it matters:** Trump's statement reduces the immediate risk of renewed large-scale strikes but leaves military escalation tied to negotiations that remain far from an agreement.

## 3. South Africa's ANC suffers historic municipal-election setback

South Africa's African National Congress won 30.76% of the municipal vote, down from 45.59% in 2021, while the Democratic Alliance received 24.64% and Jacob Zuma's uMkhonto weSizwe 9.76%. With no majority in many cities, parties have 14 days to negotiate coalitions, and the number of municipalities requiring them has more than doubled.

**Why it matters:** The result further weakens the party that has dominated South African politics since apartheid and makes fragile coalition government central to the delivery of basic municipal services.

**Topics:** [South Africa](../../Topics/South-Africa.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/south-africas-anc-battered-by-local-election-losses-ahead-coalition-talks-2026-11-08/)

## 4. Sudan's army detains ICC fugitive Ahmed Haroun

Sudan's military authorities detained Ahmed Haroun, a former senior official under Omar al-Bashir who is wanted by the International Criminal Court over alleged crimes in Darfur. His location and the reason for his detention were unclear, and neither the army nor the ICC immediately said whether he would be transferred to the court.

**Why it matters:** The detention creates a new opportunity for international accountability but also exposes the unresolved relationship between Sudan's military, former regime figures and the ongoing civil war.

## 5. Taiwan detains party leader over alleged Chinese election sponsorship

Taiwanese authorities detained Taiwan People's Communist Party chair Lin Te-wang on suspicion that he accepted Chinese Communist Party funding and instructions to run in the November 28 local elections. Prosecutors also alleged that he directed another candidate and arranged China trips offering hospitality to eligible voters; Beijing had not commented on the allegations.

**Why it matters:** The case puts alleged foreign interference at the centre of Taiwan's local campaign and tests the island's legal safeguards against political influence from Beijing.

**Topics:** [Taiwan](../../Topics/Taiwan.md), [China](../../Topics/China.md)

**Sources:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/8a5d0366b5d72e73f41bd7b9ccbb1f6d), [Central News Agency](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202611080010)

## 6. Democrats prepare investigations of Musk, Miller and major companies

Senior US Democrats said congressional investigations after they take control in January are likely to examine Elon Musk's role in federal workforce cuts, Stephen Miller's influence over immigration policy and corporate conduct affecting consumers and investors. ## 7. Switzerland enters COP31 defending existing climate commitments

Switzerland said its priority at COP31 in Antalya is to preserve and implement existing Paris Agreement commitments rather than seek major new decisions. Its delegation supports a fossil-fuel phaseout roadmap, wider climate-finance contributions from wealthy states, faster electrification, stronger natural-hazard warning systems and methane reductions.

**Why it matters:** Switzerland's position illustrates the conference's central challenge: turning existing climate promises into implementation while scientific evidence indicates the 1.5°C threshold may be temporarily exceeded.

**Topics:** [Swiss Climate Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Climate-Policy.md), [United Nations](../../Topics/United-Nations.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/various/switzerland-aims-to-ensure-progress-at-the-climate-conference/92346420)

## 8. Nvidia investigates possible foreign intelligence targeting of its CEO

Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into whether chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort after reporting linked a former business associate to Chinese intelligence. Nvidia said it had no reason to believe company secrets or US national security were compromised, while US lawmakers requested a congressional briefing.

**Why it matters:** Any compromise or leadership disruption at the dominant supplier of advanced AI chips would have consequences for technology markets, export controls and US–China security policy.

## 9. Trump signals openness to bipartisan AI safeguards

President Trump said AI risks can be managed and that he “doesn't mind guardrails,” indicating he could work with Democrats on safeguards. He did not specify measures and cautioned that regulation should not put the United States behind China, marking a limited shift from his earlier preference for industry self-regulation.

**Why it matters:** Presidential openness to safeguards could create a narrow route for federal AI policy after Democrats won Congress, although major disagreements remain over the scope of regulation.

## 10. Anthropic reportedly secures $3.4 billion data-centre power deal

Anthropic agreed to acquire commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of gas-fired generators from OnePWR Solutions in a deal worth about $3.4 billion, the Financial Times reported. Reuters could not independently verify the agreement, and neither company responded to requests for comment, but the reported purchase forms part of Anthropic's effort to secure power for expanding AI infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** The scale of the reported deal shows how AI developers are moving directly into energy procurement as grid capacity and reliable power become constraints on computing growth.
