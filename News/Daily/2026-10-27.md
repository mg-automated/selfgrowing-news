## 1. Regional powers pursue de-escalation talks in Tehran

**Why it matters:** The meetings create a regional channel for crisis management at a moment when previous attempts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and negotiate a wider settlement have failed.

## 3. Sweden's Centre leader gives up attempt to form a government

Centre Party leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist returned the government-formation mandate to parliament's speaker after a week of talks failed to produce an agreement. The four opposition parties support Social Democrat Magdalena Andersson as prime minister after their narrow election victory, but remain divided over which parties should enter government.

**Why it matters:** The impasse prolongs political uncertainty despite a clear bloc majority and tests whether Sweden's fragmented opposition can translate its election result into a stable government.

## 4. US polling gives Democrats a substantial midterm advantage

A Reuters/Ipsos poll found President Donald Trump's approval at 32%, one point above its record low, while registered voters preferred Democrats over Republicans for Congress by 47% to 36%. Cost-of-living concerns dominated the survey, and likely voters favoured Democrats by nine points one week before the November 3 election.

**Why it matters:** Control of Congress could constrain the final two years of Trump's presidency, and the poll shows economic discontent outweighing Republicans' structural advantages in key races.

## 7. Swiss court convicts Belarusian national over an enforced disappearance

The St Gallen Cantonal Court sentenced a Belarusian man to a suspended 24-month prison term for enforced disappearance linked to the 1999 abduction of opposition figures. The court overturned an acquittal in one case and relied on an offence derived from a United Nations convention that permits prosecution of acts committed abroad; the ruling can be appealed.

**Why it matters:** The judgment demonstrates how Swiss courts can use universal-jurisdiction mechanisms to pursue grave human-rights abuses committed under foreign governments.

## 9. Cadence introduces specialized AI models for chip design

Cadence launched its Maestro portfolio of domain-specific models for agentic semiconductor and system design. The company said customers could combine the models with other open or proprietary systems and deploy them in the cloud or on premises, reporting early gains of up to 30% in quality and fivefold token efficiency over general-purpose models.

**Why it matters:** Specialized, locally deployable models could accelerate chip engineering while allowing semiconductor companies to keep sensitive designs behind their own security boundaries.

## 10. Security researchers gain access to sensitive Anthropic systems

Researchers at Cathedral used AI agents to exploit weaknesses in third-party business software and reach internal Anthropic data including meeting transcripts, employment information and discussions about advanced models. Anthropic disabled the affected software and said the researchers did not reach Claude customer conversations, model code or model weights, and that it found no evidence of exploitation by others.

**Why it matters:** The incident shows how ordinary enterprise software can become a high-impact entry point into leading AI laboratories even when their core model infrastructure remains protected.
