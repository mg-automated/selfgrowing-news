## 1. Ukraine launches a major drone attack on Moscow as Russia's election ends

Ukraine launched what Moscow described as its largest-ever drone attack on the capital region on the final day of Russia's three-day parliamentary election. Russian officials said more than 1,600 drones were intercepted nationwide, including hundreds near Moscow; authorities also reported deaths in the surrounding region and a strike on an oil refinery. The Kremlin portrayed the attack as an attempt to disrupt voting, while the election is expected to preserve United Russia's dominance.

**Why it matters:** The attack links battlefield escalation directly to Russia's electoral process and gives the Kremlin a new argument for tightening control while claiming wartime legitimacy.

## 2. Russia-backed South Ossetia elects a leader closely aligned with Moscow

Marat Kambolov, a former Russian federal official, won South Ossetia's presidential election with about 85% of the vote, according to preliminary results. Moscow welcomed the result, while Georgia said Russia's support and military presence reinforce what it calls de facto annexation of the breakaway territory. Kambolov has pledged closer cooperation with Russia in defence, foreign policy and energy.

**Why it matters:** The result strengthens Moscow's political influence in a disputed Georgian territory and illustrates how Russia is consolidating control around its wider regional security perimeter.

## 3. Moldova protests Russian polling stations in Transdniestria

Moldova summoned Russia's ambassador after Moscow opened 15 polling stations in the breakaway region of Transdniestria for Russia's parliamentary election. Chisinau said the move was unacceptable and lacked oversight, while Russia accused Moldova of undermining democracy. The dispute highlights the unresolved presence of Russian forces in the separatist territory and Moldova's effort to preserve control over its reintegration process.

**Why it matters:** The polling dispute turns Russia's domestic election into another flashpoint in the security contest over Moldova and its pro-European orientation.

**Topics:** [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

## 4. Denmark and Greenland say a new US security deal will not compromise sovereignty

Denmark and Greenland said a newly reached agreement allowing a larger US military presence in Greenland will preserve the island's sovereignty and self-determination. The announcement followed President Donald Trump's claim that the arrangement gives the United States permanent control over Greenland's security, while the specific terms remain undisclosed. NATO welcomed the deal as strengthening regional security, but Greenlandic critics warned against excessive US influence.

**Why it matters:** The agreement could reshape Arctic security and the relationship among Washington, Copenhagen and Nuuk while leaving major questions about jurisdiction and strategic control unresolved.

## 6. Switzerland sets the start date for using airline passenger data in serious-crime investigations

Switzerland's Federal Council set 1 November 2026 as the entry into force of the law and ordinances governing airline passenger data. A new Passenger Information Unit at fedpol is scheduled to become operational on 1 February 2027, initially with limited staffing and scope. The government says the system will support investigations into terrorism and other serious crimes.

**Why it matters:** The decision expands Swiss law-enforcement capabilities but also creates a significant new framework for collecting, processing and sharing travel data.

## 9. Lawsuit accuses major AI companies of coordinating to slow development

A complaint filed in a US federal court accuses Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google of violating antitrust law through an alleged agreement to slow AI development in the name of safety. The plaintiffs argue that coordinated restraint reduces competition and the value delivered to users, while the companies' public positions have emphasised managing risks from increasingly capable systems. The allegations have not been proven in court.

**Why it matters:** The case could test whether safety coordination among frontier-model developers is treated as responsible risk management or as unlawful collusion.

## 10. China's CXMT says its next-generation memory platform has entered mass production

Chinese memory-chip maker CXMT said its fifth-generation platform has entered mass production, including new 24-gigabit LPDDR5X products for smartphones and other portable devices. The company says its manufacturing approach narrows memory-cell spacing and increases the number of dies produced per wafer, potentially lowering costs and power use. The advance comes as China seeks to reduce reliance on foreign semiconductor technology amid US export restrictions.

**Why it matters:** Greater domestic memory production would strengthen China's semiconductor resilience and intensify competition in a strategically important part of the AI and electronics supply chain.
