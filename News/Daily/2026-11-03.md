## 1. United States schedules Russia–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi

The United States said it would bring Ukrainian and Russian delegations together in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 for talks on ending the war, post-war security, de-escalation and humanitarian measures. US negotiators also plan consultations in Brussels with European and NATO representatives, although Kyiv and Moscow remain far apart on settlement terms.

**Why it matters:** The meeting creates a new high-level diplomatic channel just as winter approaches, but its broad agenda and the parties' unresolved differences make a rapid breakthrough uncertain.

## 2. Russian bombs kill 26 people at a Ukrainian aid site

Russian bombs struck an aid-distribution centre and neighbouring shelter in Komyshuvakha in southern Ukraine, killing 26 people, according to Ukrainian authorities. Officials said repeated strikes hit the location after people had taken cover; Russia had not publicly commented on the attack when the reports were published.

**Why it matters:** The mass-casualty attack underscores the danger to civilians near the front and sharply complicates claims that the conflict is moving toward de-escalation.

## 3. Netanyahu presses for renewed US strikes on Iran

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is urging the Trump administration to launch another wave of strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections, according to officials cited by the Associated Press. The push comes while Qatari mediators pursue a framework linking an end to Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz with relief from the US blockade of Iranian ports.

**Why it matters:** Competing military and diplomatic tracks could determine whether the regional conflict moves toward a settlement or returns to full-scale war.

**Topics:** [Israel](../../Topics/Israel.md), [Iran](../../Topics/Iran.md), [Strait of Hormuz](../../Topics/Strait-of-Hormuz.md)

## 4. United States removes South Africa and Rwanda from AGOA

Washington said South Africa and Rwanda will lose duty-free access under the African Growth and Opportunity Act on January 1, 2027. The US cited trade barriers and practices affecting national-security and foreign-policy interests; the decision threatens exports including South African vehicles, citrus and wine and Rwandan coffee and minerals.

**Why it matters:** Removing two prominent African economies from the programme deepens diplomatic rifts and may accelerate efforts to diversify trade away from the United States.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/us-suspend-south-africa-rwanda-duty-free-trade-access-2026-11-02/), [Associated Press via The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/11/02/trump-us-south-africa-rwanda-trade-agoa/3f822ed0-d6ed-11f1-930a-e08f8086f5ac_story.html)

## 5. Iliana Yotova becomes Bulgaria's first elected female president

Acting President Iliana Yotova won Bulgaria's presidential runoff with 57.6% of the vote, becoming the country's first woman elected head of state. Yotova, an ally of Prime Minister Rumen Radev who favours dialogue with Russia and opposes military aid to Ukraine, defeated pro-Western opposition candidate Andrey Gyurov.

**Why it matters:** The result strengthens Bulgaria's Russia-accommodating political camp at a time when European policy toward the war in Ukraine remains contested.

**Topics:** [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md), [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

## 6. Uganda begins withdrawing peacekeepers from Somalia

Uganda began a phased withdrawal of troops deployed in Somalia under the African Union mission, with all Ugandan forces expected to leave by the end of 2027. The drawdown starts as the UN political mission closes and Somali authorities seek greater control of security despite continuing attacks by al-Shabab.

**Why it matters:** Uganda has been a central contributor to Somalia's security for nearly two decades, and its departure will test whether Somali forces and remaining partners can contain the insurgency.

**Topics:** [United Nations](../../Topics/United-Nations.md)

**Sources:** [Associated Press via CT Insider](https://www.ctinsider.com/news/world/article/uganda-begins-withdrawing-peacekeeping-troops-22505838.php)

## 7. Switzerland prepares Lugano OSCE ministerial meeting

Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis presented priorities and preparations for the OSCE Ministerial Council scheduled for December 3–4 in Lugano, where about 1,000 participants are expected. The meeting will be the political culmination of Switzerland's 2026 OSCE chairpersonship amid severe tensions over European security.

**Why it matters:** Hosting the ministerial gives neutral Switzerland a prominent diplomatic role at a moment when the organisation's consensus-based work is under intense strain.

## 8. Germany calls AI sovereignty an existential priority

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Germany and Europe must develop more of their own artificial-intelligence capabilities to avoid dependence on outside providers. He called for faster digital modernisation across hardware, software, government and industry while acknowledging weaknesses in infrastructure and skills.

**Why it matters:** Europe's largest economy is framing domestic AI capacity as a strategic-security requirement, which could shape public investment, procurement and industrial policy.

## 9. Anthropic discloses slower growth and vast compute commitments

Anthropic's amended IPO filing showed quarterly revenue growth slowing to 47% and disclosed nearly $895.4 billion in future AI-infrastructure commitments. The company reported a narrower net loss but warned that outside financing problems at infrastructure partners could delay, reduce or raise the cost of computing capacity.

**Why it matters:** The filing gives investors an unusually detailed view of the financial risks behind frontier-AI expansion as the company prepares a potentially record-setting public offering.

## 10. ADNOC, G42 and OpenAI agree frontier-AI deployment

Abu Dhabi's ADNOC and G42 signed a multi-year agreement with OpenAI to deploy frontier AI agents across the state energy company's operations. ADNOC said it would be the first energy company to use OpenAI's platform for coordinating agents across complex business processes.

**Why it matters:** The deal extends frontier AI into critical energy operations and deepens OpenAI's commercial and strategic ties with the United Arab Emirates.
