## 1. Investigation exposes Iran's covert military procurement network

A Reuters investigation identified more than 5,000 Iranian Ministry of Defense-linked agencies used to import military and dual-use goods while evading sanctions and the US naval blockade. One agency imported $2.3 billion in goods from January 2025 through June 2026, including rocket-fuel chemicals, explosives, detonators, precision machinery, aircraft parts and trucks that can be adapted as missile or drone launchers.

**Why it matters:** The network helps explain how Iran has continued replenishing missiles and drones despite sustained military pressure, sanctions and restrictions on maritime trade.

## 2. Putin offers better European ties while warning against confrontation

Russian President Vladimir Putin said Moscow supported restoring full relations with European countries and did not want war, while adding that Russia was prepared for one and that an escalated conflict would leave no winners. He praised the Finnish and Norwegian leaders who recently called him and warned against restrictions on navigation and communications in the Baltic Sea.

**Why it matters:** Putin's combination of outreach and deterrent threats signals that renewed diplomatic contact is unlikely to reduce military pressure or disputes over European maritime security quickly.

**Topics:** [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/putin-says-moscow-seeks-better-european-ties-conflict-will-produce-no-winners-2026-10-29/)

## 3. Greece calls for a Black Sea shipping truce after another vessel sinks

Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis called for an international truce to protect Black Sea navigation after a Greek-owned container ship sank in a drone attack off Bulgaria. He also rejected the Turkey–Libya maritime-boundary agreement as illegal and urged the European Union to deploy more naval assets to its Aspides mission in the Red Sea.

**Why it matters:** Attacks on commercial shipping are raising insurance costs, threatening Ukrainian grain exports and linking European security concerns across the Black Sea, eastern Mediterranean and Red Sea.

## 4. ECB holds rates as Lagarde announces early departure

The European Central Bank kept interest rates unchanged while warning that surging energy costs were increasing inflation risks and tighter financing conditions could weigh on growth. Christine Lagarde separately announced that she would leave the ECB presidency on February 7, 2027, months before her term was due to end.

**Why it matters:** The leadership change adds uncertainty as the ECB balances energy-driven inflation, weak growth and pressure on heavily indebted European governments.

## 5. Hungary preserves US nuclear deal signed under Orbán

Prime Minister Péter Magyar's government asked parliament to make legally binding a civilian nuclear-cooperation agreement with the United States that Viktor Orbán's government signed in February. The agreement encourages the use of US technology and suppliers, including small modular reactors, while Budapest reviews whether Russia's Rosatom should remain the main contractor for expanding the Paks plant.

**Why it matters:** The decision preserves a strategic US relationship across Hungary's change of government and could reduce Russian influence over the country's future nuclear infrastructure.

**Topics:** [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md), [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

## 6. North Korean hackers blamed for a $6.3 billion Binance theft

Blockchain-analysis firm TRM Labs attributed a theft of approximately $6.36 billion from Binance wallets across ten blockchains to North Korea's TraderTraitor hacking unit. Binance said it had located and frozen about $3 billion in affected assets, while withdrawals were suspended and the full cause of the breach remained under investigation.

**Why it matters:** A theft of this scale could become the largest cryptocurrency heist on record, weaken confidence in centralised exchanges and provide an extraordinary source of funds to a sanctioned state.

**Topics:** [North Korea](../../Topics/North-Korea.md), [Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Cybersecurity.md)

## 7. Switzerland backs Yemeni tribal code to protect civilians

Switzerland supported a code of honour presented by Yemeni tribal leaders in Geneva to strengthen civilian protection, improve humanitarian access and build trust during renewed conflict. The initiative was developed by Yemeni participants and organised by the Geneva Centre for Security Sector Governance.

**Why it matters:** The code offers a locally developed mechanism for limiting harm to civilians where formal national peace efforts and military de-escalation remain fragile.

## 8. Mistral publicly releases an open cybersecurity model

Mistral released an upgraded version of its Large 4 model for anyone to download and run independently, targeting vulnerability discovery and remediation. The French company said testing with governments and businesses improved the release, while acknowledging that open weights prevent access from being contained once the model is public.

**Why it matters:** The release gives organisations more control over sensitive cybersecurity workloads but also broadens access to capabilities that can be used offensively.

## 9. Amazon and Intel results expose wider costs of the AI infrastructure boom

Amazon's heavy data-centre investment pushed cash flow lower and its holiday-quarter sales forecast missed expectations, while Intel warned that memory shortages were raising costs and weakening personal-computer demand. Alphabet and Meta also reported cash burn, and Microsoft raised $25 billion in debt as large technology companies increased infrastructure spending.

**Why it matters:** The results show AI investment straining cash flows, financing and consumer-device markets even at companies with the strongest balance sheets.

## 10. Google's custom AI-chip sales surge while inventory drains cash

Alphabet said sales of complete tensor-processing-unit systems made a meaningful contribution as Google Cloud revenue more than doubled in the September quarter. Building chips, networking and storage before customers pay has tied up cash, however, and the company expects AI infrastructure spending and TPU sales to rise sharply in 2027.

**Why it matters:** Google's expanding challenge to Nvidia demonstrates that custom accelerators can become a major cloud business, while revealing the working-capital burden of competing in AI hardware.
