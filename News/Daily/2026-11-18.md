## 1. US Senate accelerates judicial confirmations before power changes hands

The Republican-led US Senate confirmed District Judge Anna St. John to the conservative 5th Circuit Court of Appeals by 52 votes to 46 and advanced several other nominees during the lame-duck session. Republican leaders are trying to fill as many of President Donald Trump's 21 remaining judicial vacancies as possible before Democrats take control on January 3, although disputes among Republican senators delayed two Oklahoma nominees.

**Why it matters:** Lifetime judicial appointments made before the transfer of Senate control can shape federal law long after the current political majority leaves office.

## 2. Eisenkot-backed bloc takes control of Israel's parliament speakership

Israel's parliament elected Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party as speaker by 66 votes to 52, replacing Netanyahu ally Amir Ohana. The speakership gives the anti-Netanyahu bloc influence over the legislative agenda, including proposals for an independent inquiry into the October 7 attack, prime-ministerial term limits and reversal of laws that weakened judicial powers, even as Eisenkot still lacks a governing majority.

**Why it matters:** Control of the Knesset agenda gives Netanyahu's opponents institutional power before a new coalition is formed and may reshape Israel's governance even if the political deadlock continues.

## 3. Ukraine says major strike severed the road section of the Kerch Bridge

Ukrainian drones and missiles struck the bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea for a second consecutive day in what President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called an unprecedented operation. Ukraine's General Staff said an arch and road span collapsed, severing the road connection, while the railway remained standing with damage; Russia simultaneously reported a record number of Ukrainian drones and attacked bridges and infrastructure in Kyiv.

**Why it matters:** Severe damage to the strategically and symbolically important bridge could disrupt Russian logistics to Crimea and marks a significant escalation in Ukraine's long-range strike campaign.

## 4. Japan and Taiwan report Russian military aircraft activity

Japan scrambled fighters and protested after two suspected Russian aircraft violated its airspace near Okinoshima, while Taiwan said it tracked two Russian military aircraft through its air-defence identification zone east of the island. The activity occurred immediately before President Vladimir Putin's arrival in China for the APEC summit and alongside a rare visit by Russian long-range aircraft to Vietnam.

**Why it matters:** The deployment demonstrates Russia's ability to project military power in East Asia and increases the risk of miscalculation around Japan and Taiwan at a sensitive diplomatic moment.

## 5. US Navy nominee acknowledges Iran war is straining forces and weapons stocks

Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told senators that the Iran war was straining the service as lawmakers questioned long deployments, depleted interceptor and Tomahawk stocks and resources diverted from countering China. Cao confirmed an estimated daily cost of $32.5 million for the naval blockade of Iranian shipping and said weapons production was increasing, while lawmakers said rebuilding stocks and repairing damage would take years.

**Why it matters:** The testimony provides a rare public measure of the war's financial and military burden and shows how it is constraining US readiness in other strategic theatres.

## 6. UN talks expose divisions over binding rules for autonomous weapons

Delegates from 128 states meeting in Geneva began deciding whether to move toward a treaty requiring meaningful human control over lethal autonomous weapons. The United Nations has called for legally binding restrictions, while the United States, Russia and Israel have indicated opposition to binding rules and Washington has proposed a five-year process for non-binding guidelines.

**Why it matters:** The conference may determine whether international law can constrain AI-enabled weapons before their deployment becomes widespread and harder to regulate.

## 7. Swiss government backs information-sharing with firearms authorities

The Federal Council supports a parliamentary proposal allowing cantonal disability-insurance offices to alert firearms authorities when medical specialists consider an insured person dangerous and unfit to possess weapons. The proposal followed an August shooting in Aarau in which a legal gun owner receiving disability benefits killed one person and injured six; supporters say doctors rather than administrative staff should decide whether to report a case.

**Why it matters:** The measure would close an information gap between health and weapons authorities while trying to avoid treating people with mental-health conditions as a general risk.

## 8. Court clears Bern to enforce stricter short-term rental rules

Bern's Cantonal Administrative Court dismissed the final appeal against rules restricting repeated short-term rental of second homes in the old town to no more than 90 nights per year. The homeowners' association will not appeal, allowing the voter-approved measure to take effect from 2027 at the earliest after active apartment listings more than doubled from 286 in June 2022 to 623 in June 2026.

**Why it matters:** The decision clarifies how far Swiss cities may limit property and economic rights to protect long-term housing from the expansion of short-term rental platforms.

## 9. Microsoft launches specialised AI models for coding and cybersecurity

Microsoft unveiled MAI Code 2, a cybersecurity model for a limited customer group, and new image-generation and transcription models at its Ignite conference. The company says its domain-specific strategy can approach or exceed rival performance at lower cost, while Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman said safeguards and model alignment are a priority amid growing concern about AI-enabled software vulnerabilities.

**Why it matters:** Microsoft's expansion from distributing partners' models to building a specialised model portfolio increases competition with OpenAI and Anthropic and brings cybersecurity safeguards closer to the centre of commercial AI development.

## 10. Nvidia forecasts further growth after leadership upheaval

Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year, and forecast fourth-quarter revenue of about $130 billion, above analysts' average estimate. Interim chief executive Colette Kress said revenue could rise another 75% in fiscal 2028, while the company excluded China data-centre chip sales from its guidance and created a board committee on national security and export controls after Jensen Huang's departure.

**Why it matters:** The results show that demand for AI computing remains exceptionally strong despite export restrictions, a technology-sector selloff and uncertainty over leadership at the industry's most important chip supplier.
