## Overview

The consequences of war and political strain dominated the day, from Washington's 60-day review of waived Russia sanctions and a walkout during Russia's IMF remarks to new diplomatic pressure over violence in the West Bank and Lebanon. In technology policy, Anthropic urged governments to act within a narrowing window to govern increasingly capable AI.

## 1. Trump administration sets 60-day review of waived Russia sanctions

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said President Donald Trump would use up to 60 days to assess the economic effects of Russia-related sanctions and tariffs waived a day earlier. The measures under the Graham Act, including penalties for countries buying Russian energy, had been due to take effect the following week; Trump said he had not yet announced a final decision.

**Why it matters:** The review prolongs uncertainty over US pressure on Moscow and over the costs that sanctions could impose on major Russian-energy buyers.

## 2. United States presses Israel over settler violence in the West Bank

Rubio said Washington had asked Israel to do more to deter attacks by settlers in the occupied West Bank after video circulated of an assault that injured an American photojournalist and at least one other person. European diplomats and a French mayor were also attacked, and Israeli monitoring group Yesh Din counted at least 170 attacks on Palestinians since the olive harvest began on September 27.

**Why it matters:** Public US pressure adds diplomatic scrutiny to escalating settler violence and tests Israel's willingness to enforce security and legal protections in the occupied territory.

**Topics:** [Israel](../../Topics/Israel.md), [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-wants-israel-crack-down-settler-violence-west-bank-rubio-says-2026-10-17/)

## 3. Lebanon will seek an end to Israeli attacks at US-hosted talks

Lebanon said a military delegation would present Beirut's demand for an end to Israeli attacks at an October 20 trilateral meeting in Tampa, Florida. Under an earlier US-brokered framework, Lebanon would disarm Hezbollah in exchange for a gradual Israeli withdrawal, but Hezbollah has refused while Israeli forces remain on Lebanese territory and Israel continues strikes in the south.

**Why it matters:** The meeting creates a concrete diplomatic test for an agreement whose central sequencing dispute has kept the conflict unresolved.

**Topics:** [Lebanon](../../Topics/Lebanon.md), [Israel](../../Topics/Israel.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/lebanon-says-military-delegation-will-press-halt-israeli-attacks-us-talks-2026-10-17/)

## 4. Delegates walk out during Russia's remarks at IMF meeting

Nearly half of the delegates at the IMF steering committee meeting left the room when Russian Finance Minister Anton Siluanov spoke, according to Germany's central-bank president. The committee nevertheless agreed on a chair's statement warning about war, debt, energy and food insecurity, and AI-related risks while calling for continued international cooperation.

**Why it matters:** The walkout demonstrates that Russia's war against Ukraine continues to fracture economic diplomacy even when multilateral institutions manage to reach procedural agreement.

**Topics:** [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/delegates-walked-out-imf-steering-committee-meeting-when-russian-official-spoke-2026-10-17/)

## 5. Czech opposition defeats Babiš's party in Senate election

Leading Czech opposition parties won the most seats in Senate runoffs, while Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's ANO party failed to win any of the seats it contested. Babiš called the result catastrophic, although the lower house can override Senate vetoes and his eurosceptic coalition government is not expected to fall.

**Why it matters:** The result preserves an opposition majority in a chamber that approves constitutional changes and Constitutional Court nominees, constraining parts of the government's agenda.

**Topics:** [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/czech-pm-babis-suffers-setback-senate-elections-2026-10-17/)

## 6. Albanian police use rubber bullets as student protests escalate

Albanian police used rubber bullets as anti-government student protests continued in central Tirana, after earlier responses involving tear gas and water cannon. The police chief said seven officers had been suspended and faced disciplinary investigations for exceeding their authority or failing to act proportionately.

**Why it matters:** The use of more forceful crowd-control weapons and internal disciplinary action mark a new escalation in a confrontation already involving attacks on public buildings and banks.

**Topics:** [Albania](../../Topics/Albania.md), [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/albanian-anti-government-protesters-clash-with-police-tirana-2026-10-17/)

## 7. Tens of thousands protest housing and living costs in Lisbon

Tens of thousands marched through Lisbon against rising housing and living costs, increasing pressure on Portugal's centre-right government. The demonstration followed a police-cleared housing protest camp in Porto, while September inflation reached 3.6% as food and fuel prices rose.

**Why it matters:** The mobilisation shows how housing shortages and renewed inflation are turning into a broader political challenge for governments across Europe.

**Topics:** [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/tens-thousands-march-lisbon-over-housing-living-costs-2026-10-17/)

## 8. Swiss Senate committee backs 2038 Winter Olympics bid

The relevant Swiss Senate committee supported the country's planned bid for the 2038 Winter Olympic and Paralympic Games by 10 votes to none, with two abstentions, following approval in the House of Representatives. The proposal envisages up to CHF200 million in federal support, at least the same from cantons and a decentralised event without new construction.

**Why it matters:** The committee vote advances a decade-long, CHF2.2 billion project toward final parliamentary approval while defining the public contribution and governance conditions.

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch / Keystone-SDA](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-politics/committee-backing-for-the-2038-winter-olympics-in-switzerland/92231585)

## 9. Swiss children's-rights group calls for national anti-poverty strategy

The Swiss Network for Children's Rights said 114,000 children were living in poverty and another 315,000 were at risk, with single-parent and large families especially exposed. It called for binding national targets, child-specific poverty monitoring and minimum standards for family benefits to replace a patchwork that varies by canton and residency status.

**Why it matters:** The proposal turns child poverty into a federal policy question and highlights large regional differences in support and rights protection.

**Topics:** [Swiss Social Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Social-Policy.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch / SRF](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/demographics/nearly-one-in-five-children-in-switzerland-at-risk-of-poverty/92231590)

## 10. Anthropic urges faster international rules for increasingly capable AI

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark told policymakers at the IMF and World Bank meetings that rapidly improving AI could accelerate scientific discovery and, under strong assumptions, lift US output substantially by 2030. He also warned that biological and cyber misuse required strict human oversight and international cooperation, arguing that governments had only a small number of years to install effective safeguards.

**Why it matters:** The warning links AI's economic promise directly to a narrowing policy window for managing high-consequence scientific and security risks.
