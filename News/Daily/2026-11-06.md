Security risks remained acute as more tankers were damaged around the Strait of Hormuz and President Donald Trump met a Russian envoy without holding a parallel meeting with visiting Ukrainian officials. ## 1. Three more tankers are damaged around the Strait of Hormuz

Iran reported that three more tankers were damaged in the Strait of Hormuz, bringing the number of vessels reporting incidents since Sunday to at least five. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported that a vessel had been struck by two unidentified projectiles, while Qatar-mediated talks between Washington and Tehran had not produced an agreement.

**Why it matters:** Repeated incidents are keeping a critical global energy route dangerous despite diplomatic efforts and political pressure in the United States to end the war.

## 2. Trump meets Russian envoy but not visiting Ukrainian officials

President Donald Trump said he met Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev at the White House but did not meet Ukrainian officials who were visiting for the same event. Trump suggested that a prospective agreement could extend beyond Ukraine to wider European security, without disclosing what he discussed with Dmitriev.

**Why it matters:** The asymmetry of the meetings raises questions about Washington's negotiating posture days before planned Russia–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi.

## 3. Serbia certifies student movement's parliamentary majority

Serbia's election commission declared final results giving the student-led list 140 of parliament's 250 seats. The certification clears the way for a new parliament and government after the movement defeated the party of former president Aleksandar Vučić.

**Why it matters:** The result converts a protest movement's electoral victory into a governing majority and begins a major democratic transition in the western Balkans.

**Topics:** [Serbia](../../Topics/Serbia.md), [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

**Sources:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/6313af42f5226c09827d1eac8bb21b6c), [Serbia election monitor CRTA](https://crta.plus/updates/final-results-2026-parliamentary-elections-serbia/)

## 4. Patriotic Union of Kurdistan withdraws from regional government

The Patriotic Union of Kurdistan withdrew from the government of Iraq's semi-autonomous Kurdish region and threatened to form a rival administration. Party leader Bafel Talabani rejected the reconvened regional parliament as illegitimate, intensifying the long-running contest with the Kurdistan Democratic Party.

**Why it matters:** The rupture threatens the region's power-sharing system at a sensitive moment following the withdrawal of US forces and amid the wider US–Iran conflict.

**Topics:** [Iraq](../../Topics/Iraq.md), [Iran](../../Topics/Iran.md)

## 5. Nigerian court disqualifies Atiku Abubakar before presidential election

Nigeria's Federal High Court invalidated the leadership of the African Democratic Congress and the resulting nomination of former vice president Atiku Abubakar for the January 2027 presidential election. The ruling came two months before the vote and further divided the opposition facing incumbent President Bola Tinubu.

**Why it matters:** Removing a leading contender could reshape Africa's most populous country's election and provoke an urgent legal and political contest over opposition participation.

## 6. China blocks SoftBank's takeover of ABB's robotics division

China blocked SoftBank's $5.4 billion acquisition of the robotics business of Swiss-Swedish engineering group ABB. The unit has substantial Chinese operations and intellectual property, and the decision disrupts SoftBank's plan to expand in physical AI.

**Why it matters:** The intervention shows Beijing using regulatory control to retain strategically valuable robotics technology and directly affects a major Swiss industrial company.

## 7. Incoming US Senate investigator demands records from technology companies

Senator Richard Blumenthal, who is set to chair the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, demanded records from the Federal Communications Commission and companies including Apple, Meta, Google, Nvidia, Amazon and Microsoft. He said subpoenas could follow after Democrats take control in January, focusing partly on corporate donations to Trump's ballroom project and FCC actions affecting media companies.

**Why it matters:** The letters are an early indication that the incoming Congress will use investigative powers to examine links between technology companies, federal contractors and the administration.

## 8. Study finds AI is accelerating Anthropic's model development

Independent evaluator METR estimated that Anthropic's capabilities on well-specified benchmark tasks have improved about 1.9 times faster since October 2025 than during the preceding period. The study attributed the acceleration to AI's growing role in research rather than faster growth in training compute, while stressing that models still cannot autonomously handle the most important stages of training.

**Why it matters:** The findings provide concrete evidence that AI is helping accelerate its own development and may be advancing faster than evaluation and governance systems can adapt.

**Topics:** [Artificial Intelligence](../../Topics/Artificial-Intelligence.md), [AI Safety](../../Topics/AI-Safety.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/anthropics-models-improving-nearly-twice-fast-with-help-ai-study-finds-2026-11-05/)

## 9. Companies increase AI defences as cyber threats accelerate

Companies told Reuters that AI-assisted attacks and vulnerability discovery are compressing the time available to patch systems, with nearly 37,000 security flaws reported in the third quarter of 2026. Organisations including Ionos, Booking.com, Siemens and the Port of Rotterdam are expanding AI-based monitoring and defensive systems in response.

**Why it matters:** The growing use of AI by attackers and defenders is changing operational cybersecurity from periodic incident response into a continuous automated contest.

**Topics:** [Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Cybersecurity.md), [Artificial Intelligence](../../Topics/Artificial-Intelligence.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/ai-sharpens-cat-and-mouse-hacking-battle-companies-strengthen-defences-2026-11-05/)

## 10. AI sell-off pushes Nasdaq into correction territory

The Nasdaq fell 5.5% and entered correction territory as semiconductor and data-centre shares sold off sharply following the Democratic midterm victory. The Philadelphia semiconductor index briefly fell by double digits as investors reconsidered AI infrastructure spending, regulation and the valuations built around the technology boom.

**Why it matters:** The reversal shows how quickly expectations about political oversight and data-centre growth can reprice the global AI investment cycle.
