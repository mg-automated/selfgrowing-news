## Overview

Political institutions faced pressure on several fronts, from a looming EU budget confrontation and violent student unrest in Albania to consequential US election rules and closer Russian-Chinese coordination. ## 1. EU leaders enter a difficult fight over the bloc's next long-term budget

European Union leaders prepared to debate an Irish presidency proposal that would reduce the European Commission's proposed 2028–2034 budget from €1.76 trillion to €1.62 trillion. The compromise still faces opposition from net contributors and beneficiaries, while proposed cuts would fall heavily on external programmes, financial buffers, research, climate measures and infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** The eventual budget will determine how Europe balances agricultural support, competitiveness, climate investment, Ukraine assistance and repayment of pandemic-era borrowing.

## 2. Student protests in Albania escalate into widespread violence

Tens of thousands of students demonstrated in Tirana over education costs and corruption, with some protesters setting fire to the education ministry and two banks and throwing stones at police. Police used water cannons and tear gas, marking a sharp escalation after months of largely peaceful protests against Prime Minister Edi Rama's government.

**Why it matters:** The unrest transforms a sustained protest movement into a more immediate challenge to Albania's government and political stability.

**Topics:** [Albania](../../Topics/Albania.md), [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/fire-breaks-out-albanias-education-ministry-during-student-protest-2026-10-14/)

## 3. US Supreme Court leaves Republican advertising advantage in place before midterms

The US Supreme Court lifted a lower-court deadline that would have required the Federal Communications Commission to rule on a Democratic challenge to discounted broadcast advertising rates before the November 3 midterm elections. The FCC's March policy made party committees eligible for rates previously reserved for candidates, a change widely seen as helping Republicans.

**Why it matters:** The decision preserves an important campaign-finance advantage during the final weeks of an election that will determine control of Congress.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-supreme-court-again-backs-republicans-clash-over-political-ad-rates-2026-10-14/)

## 4. Russia and China coordinate positions on Ukraine and Iran

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi met at an Asian security conference in Azerbaijan and discussed bilateral cooperation, relations with the United States, and the wars involving Ukraine and Iran. Moscow said the two ministers praised their countries' strategic partnership and coordinated their political dialogue.

**Why it matters:** The meeting shows that Beijing and Moscow continue to align diplomatic positions across two conflicts with broad consequences for European and Middle Eastern security.

## 5. Switzerland plans stronger youth protections in social-media law

The Federal Council decided to expand a planned law governing communication platforms and search engines after reviewing consultation feedback. The revised proposal is expected to add protections for young users, broaden reporting procedures to all content suspected of being unlawful, strengthen user rights and require greater platform transparency, with a bill planned for June 2027.

**Why it matters:** Switzerland is moving toward broader oversight of major platforms such as Google, Facebook and YouTube as their influence over public debate grows.

## 6. Swiss government moves to extend voting rights to people with mental disabilities

The Federal Council launched consultation on legislation intended to strengthen the political rights of people with disabilities. Current federal rules remove voting rights from people under certain comprehensive guardianship arrangements because they are considered permanently unable to exercise judgment.

**Why it matters:** The proposal would modernise a long-standing restriction and broaden equal participation in Switzerland's federal democratic system.

**Topics:** [Swiss Direct Democracy](../../Topics/Swiss-Direct-Democracy.md), [Swiss Federal Council](../../Topics/Swiss-Federal-Council.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch / Keystone-SDA](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-democracy/disability-equal-political-rights-for-everyone/92213644)

## 7. Poll points to rejection of three Swiss referendum proposals

A Leewas poll commissioned by 20 Minuten and Tamedia found initial majorities against a VAT increase to finance the 13th monthly pension payment and against the initiative on taxation of married couples. Only the initiative to restrict fireworks had broad support, while the vote on changes to the War Material Act remained open.

**Why it matters:** The first voting-intention snapshot identifies the fiscal proposals facing the strongest resistance ahead of the November 29 federal ballot.

## 8. ASML reports strong orders extending into the next decade

ASML said chipmakers were already showing interest in placing orders for advanced manufacturing equipment for 2029 and 2030, indicating that AI-related capacity investment remains strong. The company reported €4.96 billion in third-quarter profit, raised its 2026 sales outlook and said previously planned capacity increases for 2027 and 2028 would not be sufficient.

**Why it matters:** ASML's order visibility is a key signal that leading chipmakers expect demand for advanced semiconductors and AI infrastructure to remain elevated for years.

## 9. Anthropic reports rapid revenue growth before anticipated IPO

Anthropic told prospective investors that third-quarter revenue rose 45% from the previous quarter to $16.8 billion and that it was profitable on an adjusted basis, according to people who attended the meeting. The investor presentation comes ahead of a highly anticipated initial public offering and amid scrutiny of the enormous infrastructure costs borne by frontier-model developers.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic's figures provide a rare indication of whether fast-growing AI revenue can begin to justify the industry's unprecedented spending commitments.

## 10. DeepMind chief calls AI-agent hacks an early warning

Google DeepMind co-founder Demis Hassabis warned that recent cyber incidents involving AI agents were early indications of potentially more serious attacks as systems become more capable. He called for stronger security practices, balanced regulation and eventually an international standards body able to respond without freezing innovation.

**Why it matters:** The warning connects demonstrated agent failures to an urgent need for technical safeguards and governance before more powerful systems are deployed widely.
