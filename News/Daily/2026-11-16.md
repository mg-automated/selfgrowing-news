## 1. Yemeni government forces retreat as Iran strikes US-linked targets in Bahrain

Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces withdrew from the Bab el-Mandeb area and Dhubab Airport to reinforce Aden and nearby fronts, reversing part of a six-week counteroffensive against the Houthis. Bahrain separately said it intercepted Iranian aerial attacks, while Iran's Revolutionary Guards said six drones had targeted a US military base in retaliation for US actions.

**Why it matters:** The retreat strengthens the Houthis' position near a critical shipping route, while renewed attacks on Bahrain risk undoing tentative US restraint toward Iran.

## 2. Swedish opposition leader wins crucial Centre Party backing

Sweden's Centre Party agreed to proceed with Social Democrat leader Magdalena Andersson's proposal for a government with the Greens, giving her an important path toward office after the September election. Andersson still needs the Left Party to tolerate the coalition, but that party continues to demand a place in government, which the Centre Party opposes.

**Why it matters:** The decision narrows Sweden's prolonged government-formation dispute but leaves a decisive conflict inside the prospective parliamentary majority unresolved.

**Topics:** [Sweden](../../Topics/Sweden.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/swedish-opposition-leader-andersson-wins-crucial-backing-form-government-2026-11-15/)

## 3. Moldova's Gagauzia heads to a runoff between two Russia-friendly candidates

Former presidential candidate Alexandr Stoianoglo won 38.44% in the election for leader of Moldova's autonomous Gagauzia region, ahead of Socialist Party candidate Fiodor Gagauz on 24.22%. Neither cleared 50%, sending them to a November 29 runoff in a region whose previous leader was imprisoned for illegally channeling Russian money to a political party.

**Why it matters:** The runoff will test relations between Moldova's pro-European central government and a strategically sensitive region where political ties to Russia remain strong.

**Topics:** [European Security](../../Topics/European-Security.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/restive-pro-russian-moldovan-region-holds-elections-2026-11-15/)

## 4. Martine Moise enters Haiti's first presidential race in a decade

Martine Moise, widow of assassinated president Jovenel Moise, registered among 33 candidates for Haiti's presidential election, whose first round is scheduled for July 2027. The vote has been repeatedly delayed as armed gangs expanded their control, and the influential Fanmi Lavalas party says conditions remain too insecure for it to field a candidate.

**Why it matters:** Her candidacy gives the planned election a prominent contender while underscoring unresolved questions about whether Haiti can hold a credible national vote amid extreme insecurity.

**Topics:** [Haiti](../../Topics/Haiti.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/martine-moise-widow-haitis-slain-president-registers-presidential-candidate-2026-11-16/)

## 5. Trump begins Asia trip under pressure after US midterm losses

US President Donald Trump headed for the Philippines and China, where meetings with Southeast Asian and Quad leaders will precede expected talks with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin at the APEC summit. ## 6. Thousands protest Geneva's proposed CHF247.7 million austerity package

Several thousand people marched peacefully in Geneva against the canton's draft 2027 budget, which contains CHF247.7 million in savings. The proposal includes 4% general subsidy cuts for associations and 5% reductions in service contracts for Geneva University Hospitals and the home-care organisation, with the cantonal parliament due to debate it in mid-December.

**Why it matters:** The turnout signals broad resistance across health, education, social and cultural services before lawmakers decide the canton's fiscal direction.

## 7. Switzerland prepares for difficult ministerial phase at COP31

**Why it matters:** The ministerial phase will determine whether states can turn technical work into political commitments and how effectively Switzerland can advance its climate priorities.

## 8. Japan's economy contracts as inflation weighs on consumption

Japan's economy shrank at an annualised 0.1% rate in the July–September quarter, its first contraction in four quarters and below forecasts for 0.3% growth. Private consumption fell 0.3% under persistent price pressure, although capital spending rose 1.6% with support from labour-saving and AI-related investment.

**Why it matters:** Weak household demand complicates Japan's recovery even as the Bank of Japan considers further rate increases to contain inflation.

## 9. SK Hynix commits $1.05 billion to domestic chip suppliers

SK Hynix said it would spend 1.4 trillion won ($1.05 billion) over five years to support South Korean suppliers of semiconductor materials, parts and equipment. It also announced a jointly funded 874.6 billion won testing facility at its Yongin complex, allowing smaller suppliers to validate products under conditions resembling commercial production.

**Why it matters:** The investment seeks to reduce supply-chain bottlenecks and strengthen South Korea's domestic semiconductor ecosystem as AI-chip competition intensifies.

## 10. Trump administration reportedly plans AI 'national champions' programme

The Wall Street Journal reported that the Trump administration plans to designate more than two dozen AI companies, including Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic, as US “national champions.” The programme would aim to help them expand overseas and compete with Chinese rivals, although Reuters said it could not independently verify the report and details of the support remain unclear.

**Why it matters:** Formal government backing could deepen the alignment between US industrial policy and leading AI companies while raising new questions about competition, export policy and geopolitical influence.
