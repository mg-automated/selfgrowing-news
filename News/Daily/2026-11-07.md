## 1. Trump names Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff

President Donald Trump named longtime adviser Stephen Miller to replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff from Monday. The appointment follows Republicans' loss of both chambers of Congress and elevates an architect of the administration's immigration crackdown as Trump prepares for Democratic investigations and greater legislative resistance.

**Why it matters:** The choice signals a confrontational response to the midterm defeat and could increase the administration's reliance on executive authority.

## 2. Taiwan closes Papua New Guinea office and halts LNG purchases

Taiwan said it would close its representative office in Papua New Guinea and stop new spot purchases of the country's liquefied natural gas, blaming Chinese pressure on Port Moresby. Taiwan said its planned purchases for the first half of 2026 were about 500,000 metric tons and represented roughly a third of Papua New Guinea's LNG exports.

**Why it matters:** Taipei is using a significant trade relationship to answer Beijing's diplomatic pressure, raising the economic cost of restricting Taiwan's international presence in the Pacific.

## 3. Sweden's Kristersson gains time to attempt government formation

Swedish caretaker prime minister Ulf Kristersson asked for more time to form a government after two opposition candidates were rejected by parliament. His prospects improved when the Sweden Democrats said they would not block him and the opposition Centre Party agreed to hear governing proposals from both Kristersson and Social Democrat leader Magdalena Andersson.

**Why it matters:** The talks offer a possible route out of Sweden's post-election deadlock, but any government still depends on bridging deep divisions over the Sweden Democrats and Left Party.

**Topics:** [Sweden](../../Topics/Sweden.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/swedens-far-right-party-gives-up-on-cabinet-posts-amid-post-election-gridlock-2026-11-06/)

## 4. Graubünden seeks to retain lower benefits for Ukrainian refugees

The Graubünden government approved a proposal that would keep social assistance for people with Ukrainian protection status S at 80% of the standard rate even after they qualify for B residence permits. The urgent bill, expected to affect about 960 people in 2027, is due to be debated by the cantonal parliament in December.

**Why it matters:** The proposal tests how cantons will handle the financial and equal-treatment consequences of Ukrainians reaching five years of residence in Switzerland.

**Topics:** [Swiss Social Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Social-Policy.md), [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md)

## 5. Switzerland prepares to resume Dublin transfers to Italy

Italy gave Switzerland written assurances that it would examine asylum cases returned under the Dublin system, and Swiss officials said the first transfers were being arranged for the coming days or weeks. Italy suspended Dublin transfers in December 2022, and an earlier Swiss effort to restart them in summer 2026 did not result in any transfers.

**Why it matters:** A functioning return channel would change Swiss asylum operations at the southern border after nearly four years of suspended Dublin cooperation.

**Topics:** [European Migration](../../Topics/European-Migration.md), [Swiss-EU Relations](../../Topics/Swiss-EU-Relations.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/foreign-affairs/italy-guarantees-switzerland-that-it-will-examine-dublin-cases/92337360), [Swiss State Secretariat for Migration background](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/sem/aktuell/italien-dublin.html)

## 6. Provisional study attributes about 1,260 Swiss deaths to summer heat

The Swiss Tropical and Public Health Institute provisionally attributed 1,257 deaths between May and August to high temperatures during Switzerland's hottest recorded summer. People aged 75 and older accounted for about 85% of the estimated deaths, while final figures are expected in spring 2027.

**Why it matters:** The estimate puts a national mortality measure on extreme heat and strengthens the evidence base for Swiss climate-adaptation and public-health policy.

**Topics:** [Swiss Climate Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Climate-Policy.md), [Swiss Health Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Health-Policy.md)

**Sources:** [Swiss Federal Office of Public Health](https://www.bag.admin.ch/de/hitzebedingte-sterblichkeit), [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/climate-adaptation/summer-heatwave-linked-to-over-1200-deaths-in-switzerland/92338508)

## 7. US Democrats prepare broad AI and data-centre legislation

Democratic lawmakers said they plan bills on data-centre electricity costs, independent safety testing of advanced AI models and liability for harm caused by AI systems after taking control of Congress in January. President Trump could veto the measures, but committee control will let Democrats compel testimony and intensify scrutiny of major technology companies.

**Why it matters:** The agenda could shift US AI policy from promoting rapid development toward mandatory oversight, even if partisan conflict blocks immediate legislation.

## 8. AI demand turns advanced chip packaging into a strategic bottleneck

Semiconductor companies are committing billions of dollars to advanced packaging that combines processors and memory into high-performance units. TSMC dominates leading capacity, while Nvidia is financing expansion at ASE's SPIL unit and Powertech is developing panel-level packaging with AMD and Broadcom as customers seek additional suppliers.

**Why it matters:** Packaging capacity has become a key constraint on AI computing, widening the semiconductor contest beyond fabrication plants and influencing where future infrastructure investment flows.

## 9. European Commission preliminarily finds TikTok feed breaches digital rules

The European Commission preliminarily found that TikTok's personalised “For You” feed breaches the Digital Services Act because its design can drive users into repeated streams of harmful content. The findings focus on TikTok's risk assessment and mitigation measures, particularly for vulnerable adults and minors exposed to content about mental health and body image.

**Why it matters:** The case applies the EU's platform-safety regime directly to the design of a major recommendation algorithm and could require product changes rather than only content moderation.

**Sources:** [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-you-feed-breach-digital-services-act-harmful-rabbit-holes)

## 10. Japan announces emergency cybersecurity package

Japan announced a ¥10 billion emergency package after a series of breaches hit companies and government agencies. The plan includes AI-assisted defensive teams, a public-private cyber rescue unit and expanded use of active cyber defence, with a specialist team due to be established by the end of November.

**Why it matters:** The package combines national-security powers, incident response and AI tools into a more interventionist model for protecting critical digital systems.
