## 1. Serbia votes in watershed challenge to Vučić's rule

Serbians began voting in a snap parliamentary election in which a student-led movement is mounting the strongest challenge yet to long-time leader Aleksandar Vučić. The campaign grew from nearly two years of protests after the fatal Novi Sad station-canopy collapse, while polls indicated a close contest between the students and Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party.

**Why it matters:** The result could reshape Serbia's domestic institutions and its balancing act between the European Union, Russia and China.

**Why it matters:** The attacks show Ukraine sustaining pressure on Russia's industrial and defence supply base even as limited diplomatic contacts continue.

## 5. Gunmen kill at least 40 people in northeastern Nigeria

Gunmen killed at least 40 people in an attack on Binnari village in Nigeria's Taraba state, according to Amnesty International and police. Authorities said a large armed group carried out the assault on Friday afternoon.

**Why it matters:** The killings underscore the persistent civilian-security crisis confronting Africa's most populous country as it enters an election campaign.

## 7. Britain plans new borrowing powers for English mayors

Britain's government said the coming budget would give English mayors access to a £1.5 billion borrowing facility and £285 million in seed funding for locally chosen growth projects. Strategic authorities are also due to receive funding to expand their capacity as more budget responsibilities move away from Westminster.

**Why it matters:** The package would materially decentralize investment decisions and could change how housing, transport and regional regeneration are financed in England.

**Topics:** [United Kingdom](../../Topics/United-Kingdom.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/uk-budget-set-hand-english-mayors-new-borrowing-powers-growth-funding-2026-10-24/)

## 8. Audi announces $4 billion US factory in Georgia

Audi said it plans a $4 billion assembly plant in Georgia with capacity for 200,000 SUVs a year and about 4,500 jobs. The announcement came at a campaign rally where President Donald Trump presented the investment as evidence that his tariffs were shifting vehicle production to the United States.

**Why it matters:** The project is a major test of whether tariff-driven industrial policy produces durable domestic manufacturing gains despite higher costs and trade uncertainty.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md), [Germany](../../Topics/Germany.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/trump-set-announce-plan-by-audi-build-us-factory-georgia-source-says-2026-10-24/), [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/audi-georgia-trump-manufacturing-auto-plant-c1d49f2437c1d9cf349af399b8abe3a3)

## 9. Security vendors report widespread attempts to exploit critical PHP flaws

Security companies reported broad exploitation attempts against two critical PHP vulnerabilities, with millions of potentially exposed systems and evidence of attempts to steal credentials and penetrate corporate networks. **Why it matters:** The incident has moved from emergency patching to a large-scale threat-hunting problem affecting software that underpins much of the web.

## 10. Indonesia cuts gas exports to Singapore to supply domestic power

Indonesia reduced pipeline natural-gas exports to Singapore by about 9%, diverting 31 million standard cubic feet per day to its domestic power sector. The regulator said the reduction began on Saturday.

**Why it matters:** The move highlights how domestic energy-security priorities can quickly affect neighboring economies that depend on cross-border fuel supplies.
