## 1. Flávio Bolsonaro challenges Brazil's election result from the United States

After arriving in Miami, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro declared himself Brazil's rightful leader and alleged that President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and the Supreme Court had stolen Sunday's runoff. Brazil's electoral authority, international observers, global leaders and Bolsonaro's own party recognised Lula's roughly 4.5-million-vote victory, and Bolsonaro offered no evidence of fraud.

**Why it matters:** A defeated candidate's rejection of a certified result from abroad tests Brazil's institutions and complicates relations with Washington, which denies helping the Bolsonaro family return to power.

## 2. Taiwan reports a wide deployment of more than 50 Chinese vessels

Taiwan's national security chief said 53 Chinese navy and other official vessels, including 34 warships and the aircraft carrier *Liaoning*, were operating from waters south of Japan to areas around Taiwan and east of the Philippines. Japan separately tracked Chinese ships moving through the Osumi Strait, while Beijing described the carrier's activity as routine training.

**Why it matters:** The simultaneous formations increase military pressure across several regional flashpoints and test the monitoring and deterrence capacity of Taiwan, Japan and the Philippines.

## 3. North Korea fires a possible ballistic missile toward the sea

North Korea launched a projectile toward the sea off its east coast, according to South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff. Japanese authorities said it may have been a ballistic missile and had already fallen, following North Korea's previous missile launches on October 12 amid heightened inter-Korean tension.

**Why it matters:** The launch adds another military signal during a period of border tension and on the day Switzerland's foreign minister is due to reopen the Swiss diplomatic office in Pyongyang.

## 4. Kremlin casts doubt on proposed November Ukraine talks

Kremlin foreign policy adviser Yuri Ushakov said Russia had not proposed a date for renewed three-way talks with Ukraine and the United States, despite Kyiv's hope for a November 6–10 meeting in Abu Dhabi. He cited the Middle East conflict, battlefield conditions and the broader situation, and said European participation had not been discussed.

**Why it matters:** Moscow's comments weaken expectations that the Oleshky humanitarian ceasefire will quickly lead to broader negotiations and underline Europe's continued exclusion from the US-led channel.

## 5. Canada and the European Union narrow plans for closer ties

Canada and the European Union agreed to pursue an "ever closer association" but removed the undefined and divisive idea of Canadian associate membership from a draft summit declaration. The text proposes work on subsea cables, a study of Canadian liquefied-natural-gas exports, youth mobility and compatible AI and digital rules, with negotiations continuing into next year.

**Why it matters:** The compromise advances Canada's effort to reduce its dependence on the United States while avoiding a label that several EU governments considered legally unclear.

## 6. Federal Reserve holds rates as Warsh rejects political influence

The US Federal Reserve left its policy rate unchanged at 3.75%–4.00%, with two officials dissenting, and Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role in the decision. The hold came days before congressional elections and amid President Donald Trump's pressure for lower rates and continuing efforts to remove Governor Lisa Cook.

**Why it matters:** The decision and divided vote put the central bank's inflation judgment and institutional independence under scrutiny at a politically sensitive moment.

## 7. Swiss government advances its AI-regulation framework

The Federal Council reviewed interim work to implement the Council of Europe AI Convention, including an inventory of federal AI measures and guidance for voluntary industry codes. Legally binding rules on transparency, data protection, non-discrimination, supervision and risk assessment are being drafted for consultation in spring 2027.

**Why it matters:** Switzerland is defining how voluntary sector rules and future legislation will divide responsibility for governing AI across the economy and federal administration.

## 8. Switzerland evaluates Israeli and French long-range air-defence systems

The Federal Council instructed the defence ministry to evaluate Israel's Barak MX and France's SAMP/T NG as possible additions to the US-made Patriot system. It cited the deteriorating security environment and the need to reduce reliance on one supplier or supply chain, while continuing the delayed Patriot procurement.

**Why it matters:** The review could diversify a CHF6-billion air-defence programme and reshape Switzerland's defence-industrial relationships amid pressure on European missile inventories.

## 9. Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon for cybersecurity work

Google unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model designed for vulnerability discovery, exploitation, patching and long-running engineering tasks. The company said it generates secure code at more than twice Gemini 4 Argon's success rate on internal benchmarks and is initially limiting access to selected defenders through its Fairwind programme while safeguards are tested.

**Why it matters:** A model capable of both finding and exploiting vulnerabilities highlights the dual-use stakes of frontier AI and Google's attempt to control deployment through staged access.

## 10. Samsung warns the AI memory-chip shortage will deepen

Samsung Electronics forecast that AI-driven memory demand will outstrip industry supply even more sharply in 2027 after quarterly operating profit rose nearly ninefold. The company plans to raise 2026 chip capital spending by 62% to 76.8 trillion won and increase it again next year, although higher memory costs pushed its mobile business into a quarterly loss.

**Why it matters:** The results show AI demand driving extraordinary semiconductor investment while transmitting higher component costs to consumer-device businesses.
