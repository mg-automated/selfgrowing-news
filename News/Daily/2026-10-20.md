## 1. Trump says Ukraine should replace Zelenskiy as its negotiator

US President Donald Trump said someone other than Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy should negotiate for Kyiv, deepening a public rift between the two leaders. US envoy Steve Witkoff was separately expected to join Ukraine talks in Oslo and then travel to Russia to meet President Vladimir Putin.

**Why it matters:** Open US pressure on Ukraine's elected leadership could alter Kyiv's negotiating leverage and strain coordination among its Western supporters.

## 2. Trump signals openness to three-way nuclear talks with Xi and Putin

Trump said he was open to meeting Chinese President Xi Jinping and Putin during next month's APEC summit in China. The Kremlin said a trilateral meeting was possible but not agreed, while the proposed agenda would centre on nuclear arms control amid uncertainty following the expiry of New START.

**Why it matters:** A meeting among the three largest nuclear powers could reopen arms-control diplomacy, but it also raises concerns among allies about deals negotiated without them.

## 3. France imposes local curfews after riots

French authorities imposed curfews in parts of Paris, Lille, Nice and other cities after a weekend of rioting and looting linked to nationwide anti-government protests. President Emmanuel Macron rejected the interior minister's offer to resign, while unions called national strikes for November 5.

**Why it matters:** The move marks a material escalation from appeals for calm and adds political and fiscal pressure on the French government.

## 4. US orders DeepSeek removed from civilian government systems

The US Department of Homeland Security ordered civilian federal agencies to remove DeepSeek products and third-party services using the Chinese model from their IT systems. The notice cited risks involving data transfers to China, intelligence collection and potential malware or vulnerabilities.

**Why it matters:** The order broadens Washington's technology restrictions from hardware and telecommunications into widely used AI models and services.

## 5. Anthropic and Nscale discuss another $45 billion AI cloud deal

Nvidia-backed Nscale and Anthropic were in talks over a second computing agreement that could be worth about $45 billion, according to a person familiar with the matter. The proposed West Virginia facility would provide 460 megawatts of capacity using Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips, although terms remained under negotiation.

**Why it matters:** The prospective deal illustrates the extraordinary scale of AI infrastructure commitments even as investors question debt, power availability and returns.

**Why it matters:** Intrusive spyware aimed at political opponents can compromise election integrity and suppress democratic participation even when infections are unsuccessful.

## 7. Tanker mine blast adds pressure to Strait of Hormuz diplomacy

A tanker caught fire and exploded after striking a naval mine in the southern Strait of Hormuz, according to Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy as reported by Iranian media. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Tehran's offer to reopen the waterway remained available and called for a regional security arrangement without foreign forces.

**Why it matters:** Another tanker incident increases risks to a critical energy route while diplomacy over reopening it remains stalled.

**Why it matters:** The decisions show Switzerland weighing advanced analytics against data sovereignty and security risks in defence procurement.
