## 1. US and Iran hold separate talks with mediators on an amended proposal

US and Iranian officials held separate discussions with mediators over an amended seven-day proposal aimed at de-escalating the conflict, according to officials familiar with the process. The issues included a ceasefire, sanctions and frozen assets, reopening the Strait of Hormuz and a path back to nuclear negotiations, but major differences remained.

**Why it matters:** The talks reopen a diplomatic channel after Washington rejected Tehran's earlier offer, although the scope and sequencing of concessions remain significant obstacles.

## 2. Sweden faces political deadlock after Andersson abandons formation attempt

Social Democratic leader Magdalena Andersson said she could not form a government despite the centre-left winning a narrow parliamentary majority in Sweden's September 13 election. Parliamentary speaker Andreas Norlén is due to consult party leaders after the Left Party withheld the support needed for Andersson's bid.

**Why it matters:** The impasse could shift the next formation attempt to the centre-right and, if four candidates fail, ultimately trigger another election.

## 3. Ukraine uses heavy-lift drones to supply civilians in occupied Oleshky

Ukraine said heavy-lift drones had delivered about four tonnes of food and medical supplies to roughly 100 locations in Russian-occupied Oleshky, where officials estimate around 2,000 civilians remain. Ukrainian officials said residents lack basic services and Russia has rejected evacuation proposals; the Kremlin acknowledged severe shortages but disputed responsibility.

**Why it matters:** The operation shows drones being used to bypass front lines for humanitarian relief where conventional access and civilian evacuation are blocked.

**Topics:** [Ukraine](../../Topics/Ukraine.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/ukraine-uses-drones-deliver-food-russian-occupied-oleshky-2026-09-28/)

## 4. Lebanon seeks a new staff-level agreement with the IMF

Lebanon's government said it hoped to reach a new staff-level agreement with the International Monetary Fund as it tries to revive a reform programme for its long-running financial crisis. Progress depends in part on parliament approving legislation to distribute losses in the banking system and close the country's financial gap.

**Why it matters:** An IMF agreement could unlock external financing, but only if Lebanon resolves politically difficult questions about who bears the banking system's losses.

**Topics:** [Lebanon](../../Topics/Lebanon.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/lebanon-hopes-for-new-staff-level-agreement-with-imf-2026-09-28/)

## 5. Swiss Senate opens marathon debate on EU treaty package

Switzerland's Council of States began debating the Bilaterals III package governing future relations with the European Union. The Social Democrats warned that centre-right changes on wage protection, immigration and accompanying measures could endanger the package, while the Swiss People's Party continued to reject it outright.

**Why it matters:** The parliamentary decisions will shape both the domestic coalition behind the treaties and the terms voters may eventually be asked to approve.

**Topics:** [Swiss–EU Relations](../../Topics/Swiss-EU-Relations.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/foreign-affairs/senate-begins-marathon-debate-on-swiss-eu-treaty-package/92132303)

## 6. Employee data stolen in cyberattack on Swiss public broadcaster

The Swiss Broadcasting Corporation said employee data belonging to its German-language unit SRF was stolen in a cyberattack. The broadcaster said it had found no indication that the information had been published or misused and was investigating the incident.

**Why it matters:** The breach exposes the continuing vulnerability of nationally important media organisations and their staff data to cyber intrusion.

## 7. AMD agrees to acquire World Labs for $8.2 billion

AMD agreed to buy Fei-Fei Li's World Labs in an $8.2 billion transaction, expanding beyond chips into spatial-intelligence models designed to understand and generate three-dimensional environments. The acquisition gives AMD a larger role in so-called physical AI as it competes for spending beyond conventional data-centre accelerators.

**Why it matters:** The deal shows semiconductor companies moving up the AI stack to control models and software that could power robotics, simulation and embodied systems.

## 8. Nvidia releases safeguards for autonomous AI agents

Nvidia released OpenShell and Sentry, security tools intended to constrain autonomous AI agents and detect dangerous behaviour. The company said the software could have prevented a recent attack involving malicious code hosted on Hugging Face by isolating agents and monitoring their actions.

**Why it matters:** As agents gain access to files, networks and tools, enforceable runtime controls are becoming as important as model-level safety policies.

## 9. Anthropic IPO filing sets out sweeping ambitions and surging costs

Anthropic's IPO prospectus described plans to expand its Claude models across consumer, enterprise and scientific uses while revealing rapidly rising computing and operating costs. The filing gave public investors a detailed view of the capital intensity behind frontier-model development.

**Why it matters:** The prospectus turns the economics of a leading AI laboratory into a public-market test of whether fast revenue growth can justify extraordinary infrastructure spending.

## 10. Samsung commits $1 billion to Helix Digital's AI buildout

Samsung Electronics and affiliated companies committed $1 billion to KKR-backed Helix Digital's expansion of AI data-centre infrastructure. The investment links a major memory-chip supplier more closely to the construction of computing capacity for AI workloads.

**Why it matters:** The commitment illustrates how chipmakers, infrastructure investors and data-centre operators are forming deeper financial partnerships to fund the AI capacity boom.
