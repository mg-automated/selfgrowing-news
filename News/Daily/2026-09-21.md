## 1. United Russia dominates early results in tightly controlled parliamentary election

Early results from Russia's first parliamentary election since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine showed United Russia with nearly 58% of the party-list vote after about 58% of precincts had reported, while it was also positioned to win most single-seat races. Genuine opposition was largely absent, voting was held in occupied Ukrainian territories, and opposition groups reported violations that could not all be independently verified.

**Why it matters:** The result is a material conclusion to the election covered earlier in the archive and is set to preserve the Kremlin's legislative dominance during the war.

## 2. Berlin's Left Party wins state election on a housing-nationalisation platform

The Left Party won Berlin's state election with 25.3% of the vote, ahead of the CDU on 19%, the AfD on 16% and the SPD on 12.1%. Lead candidate Elif Eralp pledged to pursue legislation that could transfer about 220,000 privately owned homes into public ownership, although coalition talks and the policy's cost remain major obstacles.

**Why it matters:** The result removes Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU from the lead in the capital and shows voters shifting toward both ends of Germany's political spectrum.

**Topics:** [Germany](../../Topics/Germany.md)

## 3. Trump and Zelenskyy agree to meet at the UN General Assembly

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he and US President Donald Trump agreed to meet in New York during the UN General Assembly, describing new diplomatic momentum toward ending the war. Zelenskyy said their teams were working on de-escalation and security issues including energy, food and civilian protection, but no concrete agreement was announced.

**Why it matters:** A leader-level meeting could influence the diplomatic track and future US support at a consequential stage of the Russia-Ukraine war.

## 4. Michelle Bachelet withdraws from the race to lead the United Nations

Former Chilean president and UN human-rights chief Michelle Bachelet withdrew her candidacy to succeed António Guterres as UN secretary-general after her support among Security Council members weakened. Her departure leaves seven declared candidates, with Costa Rica's Rebeca Grynspan leading the latest informal straw poll.

**Why it matters:** The withdrawal materially reshapes the contest for leadership of the United Nations as it confronts wars, institutional strain and pressure on multilateral cooperation.

## 5. Macron backs Canada becoming the EU's first associate member

French President Emmanuel Macron endorsed Canada's proposed status as the European Union's first associate member during talks with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney in Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon. The leaders also agreed to deepen cooperation in defence, energy, space, telecommunications and emerging technologies as Canada seeks greater strategic resilience through closer European ties.

**Why it matters:** The proposal could create a new model of EU partnership and mark a significant realignment in Canada's traditionally US-centred foreign and economic policy.

## 7. Australia launches a bid for a 2029–2030 UN Security Council seat

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced that Australia will seek one of the two non-permanent Security Council seats assigned to its electoral group for 2029–2030. Australia expects support from Pacific and Southeast Asian countries and will compete alongside Finland, with the election scheduled for 2028.

**Why it matters:** A successful bid would return Australia to the Security Council for the first time since 2014 and give it a formal role in decisions on peace, security, climate-related risks and emerging technology.

## 8. China slows humanoid-robot listings as regulators scrutinise the investment boom

Chinese regulators have informally raised the bar for humanoid-robot initial public offerings while examining whether high valuations and revenue from state-backed projects reflect sustainable commercial demand, according to Reuters. **Why it matters:** Beijing is trying to contain speculative excess without undermining robotics, a technology it has designated as a national priority.

## 9. Taiwan breaks ground on advanced-packaging park anchored by TSMC

Taiwan began construction of the Baipu Industrial Park in Kaohsiung, where TSMC plans a technology-validation laboratory and training centre expected to start operating in late 2029. The park is designed to help equipment and materials suppliers test technologies used in advanced packaging for high-performance AI chips.

**Why it matters:** Advanced packaging has become a critical constraint in AI-chip production, and the investment strengthens Taiwan's central position in the global semiconductor supply chain.

## 10. First industry-wide tally shows humanoid robots remain a small commercial market

The International Federation of Robotics counted about 7,000 humanoid robots sold worldwide in 2025 for industrial and professional-service use, one of the first broad measurements of the sector. Many were purchased for research or AI training rather than productive work, compared with hundreds of thousands of conventional industrial and service robots sold annually.

**Why it matters:** The data provides a reality check on a heavily funded field whose demonstrations and forecasts remain far ahead of current commercial deployment.

**Topics:** [Robotics](../../Topics/Robotics.md), [Artificial Intelligence](../../Topics/Artificial-Intelligence.md)
