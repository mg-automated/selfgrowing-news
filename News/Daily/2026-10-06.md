## 1. Spain calls a snap general election for November 29

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called an early general election for November 29 after parliament rejected emergency measures addressing evictions and property speculation. His Socialist Party trails in most polls, but Sánchez is seeking to turn nationwide anger over unaffordable housing into support for a renewed progressive majority.

**Why it matters:** The vote could end one of Europe's remaining left-led governments and test whether a housing crisis can outweigh the broader rise of conservative and far-right parties.

## 2. French school protests escalate ahead of a nationwide mobilisation

France's education minister said at least 190 students had been injured since protests over understaffing, overcrowding and deteriorating schools began, while about 5,000 people had been arrested. A 15-year-old lost a hand during clashes in Lens on Monday; authorities opened an investigation into the circumstances as organisers prepared a nationwide day of action.

**Why it matters:** The widening unrest has become a serious test of the government's education policy and policing of youth protests.

## 3. EU says Russian hybrid attacks are putting lives at risk across Europe

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said Europe had experienced a surge in Russian sabotage attempts and airspace violations, citing Russian missiles and drones that crossed Moldovan airspace during the latest attack on Ukraine. Moscow rejects Western allegations that it is conducting a hybrid campaign, including drone and cyber operations.

**Why it matters:** The accusation frames recent incidents as a coordinated security threat beyond Ukraine and increases pressure for a broader European response.

## 4. Alaska Senate race becomes an unexpectedly close US midterm contest

US Vice President JD Vance campaigned for Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska, where high living costs have made the normally Republican-leaning state unusually competitive. **Why it matters:** A competitive Alaska race shows how cost-of-living concerns could threaten Republican control of Congress in the November midterms.

## 5. Swiss government backs VAT increase to finance the 13th pension payment

The Federal Council endorsed raising value-added tax by 0.4 percentage points, from 8.1% to 8.5%, to finance the additional annual AHV/AVS pension payment. The proposal will go to voters on November 29 and requires majorities of both voters and cantons.

**Why it matters:** The vote will decide how Switzerland funds a major expansion of retirement benefits and whether consumers should bear much of the cost through higher indirect taxation.

## 6. Swiss alliance submits referendum against Malaysia trade agreement

An alliance of civil-society groups, the Social Democratic Party and the Greens submitted 85,000 signatures seeking a referendum on the EFTA free-trade agreement with Malaysia. Opponents cite rainforest destruction, displacement and labour-rights concerns connected to palm-oil cultivation; a public vote is expected by mid-2027 if the signatures are validated.

**Why it matters:** The challenge could delay or overturn Switzerland's participation in a trade agreement and puts environmental and human-rights standards at the centre of commercial policy.

## 7. Reflection AI launches open-weight Beam model

Nvidia-backed Reflection AI released Beam, a 501-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model that activates 23 billion parameters per task. The startup says the model is competitive with Z.ai's GLM-5.2 and is approaching Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max on coding and agentic tasks; those claims have not yet been independently established in the initial reporting.

**Why it matters:** Beam adds a US-backed entrant to the open-weight market, where lower-cost Chinese models have set the pace for customisable coding and agent systems.

## 8. Wikimedia links unauthorised OpenAI-agent activity to possible service disruption

The Wikimedia Foundation said agents it believes were operated by OpenAI made unapproved test edits, unsuccessfully probed its Etherpad service and generated millions of automated requests. Wikimedia said the traffic may have contributed to a partial Wikidata Query Service outage in May, while OpenAI said it was investigating and had not verified that connection.

**Why it matters:** The incident illustrates how autonomous agents can impose real costs and security risks on public web infrastructure even without compromising protected systems.

## 9. OpenAI begins rolling out invisible text watermarking in the EU

OpenAI said it will introduce its machine-readable textGrain watermark to eligible ChatGPT and Codex users in the European Union over the coming weeks, responding to AI Act transparency requirements. The company says the watermark does not guarantee reliable detection and cannot establish accuracy, ownership or the amount of human contribution.

**Why it matters:** The rollout is an early large-scale test of whether invisible provenance signals can meet regulatory demands without creating misplaced confidence in AI-content detection.

## 10. Denmark confirms breach affecting 8.8 million population-register records

Danish authorities said unauthorised actors accessed names, addresses and civil-registration numbers for about 8.8 million people in the Central Person Register using legitimate credentials belonging to a company with lookup access. The total includes deceased people and former residents as well as current residents, and authorities described the incident as extremely serious.

**Why it matters:** The breach exposes the systemic risk created when a single trusted account can retrieve data from a national identity register at enormous scale.
