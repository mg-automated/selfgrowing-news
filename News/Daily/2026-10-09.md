## 1. Russian attacks kill 78 people across Ukraine in two days

A Russian guided bomb and subsequent drone attack killed 35 people in Kramatorsk, including passengers near two buses, Ukrainian authorities said. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian strikes had killed 78 people and wounded 215 across Ukraine in two days; attacks in Kyiv killed four people and interrupted water and electricity supplies.

**Why it matters:** The new attacks extend the previous day's mass-casualty barrage and show Russia intensifying pressure on civilians and essential services as winter approaches.

## 2. Trump rules out attacking Iran before US midterm elections

US President Donald Trump said the United States would not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm elections and described bilateral discussions as productive. The two countries have exchanged proposals aimed at ending the war and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, but Tehran reiterated that it would not abandon uranium enrichment or surrender its uranium stockpile.

**Why it matters:** The public assurance creates a short de-escalation window, although the unresolved nuclear and shipping disputes leave the longer-term risk of renewed fighting intact.

## 3. Britain targets Russia's shadow fleet and financial channels with new sanctions

Britain imposed 38 new Russia-related sanctions covering oil transport, military manufacturing and financial-sanctions evasion. The package included 12 tankers identified as part of Russia's shadow fleet, three cryptocurrency exchanges, two payment platforms and 17 suppliers of goods used to produce missiles and drones.

**Why it matters:** The measures broaden pressure from oil revenues to the payment and procurement networks that sustain Russia's war economy.

## 4. US and Russian officials explore American investment in Nord Stream

Senior US and Russian officials have discussed bringing an American investor into the sanctioned Nord Stream gas pipelines, according to Reuters reporting based on five people familiar with the matter. A US official said a deal was unlikely in the medium term, while Germany said it had replaced Russian gas and did not want the pipelines reopened.

**Why it matters:** The discussions expose a sharp tension between commercial diplomacy with Moscow and Europe's strategy of reducing energy dependence on Russia during the war.

## 5. Myanmar's military-backed president seeks closer ties with Malaysia

Myanmar President Min Aung Hlaing met Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim on his fifth bilateral visit to an ASEAN country since taking office after a widely criticised election. Malaysia offered closer economic cooperation while raising concerns about democratic reform and violence, even as its return of nearly 1,500 Myanmar nationals drew criticism from the UN refugee agency and rights groups.

**Why it matters:** Bilateral engagement may help Myanmar's military-backed government gain regional legitimacy while weakening ASEAN's collective leverage over the civil war and democratic transition.

## 6. Cyberattack leaks data linked to Switzerland's federal pension fund

Switzerland's federal pension fund Publica said data was leaked after an external software supplier suffered a cyberattack in late September. The affected information and supplier were not identified, and the Office of the Attorney General opened an investigation while Publica notified its approximately 112,000 active members and pensioners.

**Why it matters:** The breach demonstrates how third-party software can expose sensitive data held by a major federal institution even when core government systems are not directly compromised.

## 7. Swiss wolf-culling permits rise sharply after rule change

The Federal Office for the Environment had issued legally binding preventive-culling decisions for 39 wolf packs by early autumn, up from 22 at the same point in 2025. The increase reflects both a larger wolf population and an amended Hunting Ordinance that allows permits for wolf pups before their presence has been confirmed, although culling can occur only after confirmation.

**Why it matters:** The figures show how recent regulatory changes are accelerating Switzerland's shift toward more active wolf-population management.

**Topics:** [Swiss Wildlife Policy](../../Topics/Swiss-Wildlife-Policy.md)

**Sources:** [SWI swissinfo.ch / Keystone-SDA](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/various/permits-for-the-culling-of-wolves-as-a-preventative-measure-have-risen-sharply/92184939)

## 8. Google Cloud launches a cross-platform Gemini workplace agent

Google Cloud introduced a Gemini agent that can plan work, use tools and produce finished outputs across Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Slack. The system can select between Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude models, and organisations can create restricted “coworker agents” with their own email addresses and limited data access.

**Why it matters:** The launch moves autonomous agents deeper into everyday business systems and intensifies competition over which platform will mediate knowledge work.

## 9. French group plans €10 billion AI data centre in Finland

French AI-infrastructure company Sesterce said it plans to develop a data-centre site in central Finland costing more than €10 billion. The first 200-megawatt phase is expected to expand to 600 MW, with a longer-term goal of more than one gigawatt of Finnish capacity and an anchor customer already secured.

**Why it matters:** The project would make Finland a larger European AI-computing hub and illustrates the scale of capital and electricity now required for frontier infrastructure.

## 10. Flexible AI data-centre power use gains momentum in the United States

US regulators, utilities and technology companies are increasingly exploring demand-response programmes that reduce or shift data-centre electricity use during grid stress. Industry research estimates that participating facilities could cut peak demand by 10% to 30% and avoid $40 billion to $150 billion in capital investment over a decade, though most projects remain pilots or one-off agreements.

**Why it matters:** Flexible consumption could allow AI infrastructure to grow faster without imposing the full cost of new generation and grid upgrades on electricity systems and consumers.
