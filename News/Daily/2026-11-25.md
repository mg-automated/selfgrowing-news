## 1. Houthis threaten Saudi Red Sea ports after drone strike on Riyadh

Yemen's Houthis threatened to target Jeddah and every other Saudi port on the Red Sea if the Saudi-led coalition attacked Hodeidah, the main entry point for food and aid into Houthi-controlled northern Yemen. The warning followed a Houthi drone strike on Riyadh's tallest tower that wounded at least 11 people; the coalition subsequently said it struck Houthi capabilities in Hodeidah, Saada and Amran.

**Why it matters:** The exchange broadens the conflict toward civilian and commercial infrastructure around a crucial shipping corridor and raises the risk to humanitarian imports for millions of Yemenis.

## 2. Eisenkot secures first coalition agreement in bid to lead Israel

Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party agreed a coalition pact with Avigdor Lieberman's Israel Our Home, the first formal alliance since Israel's October election. Eisenkot still lacks a majority and is simultaneously negotiating with other opposition parties and Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud before his December 6 deadline to form a government.

**Why it matters:** The agreement is the first concrete step toward replacing Netanyahu, but the fragmented parliament leaves Israel's next government and its policy direction unresolved.

## 3. Russian strike kills two and wounds dozens in Zaporizhzhia

A Russian glide-bomb attack on Zaporizhzhia killed two civilians and wounded 48 people, including four children, while damaging homes and an apartment building. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine was reinforcing the region and needed another €25 billion in foreign assistance next year beyond the €90 billion already assured.

**Why it matters:** The attack shows the continuing civilian cost and pressure on Ukraine's southern front as Kyiv seeks additional military and financial support for 2027.

## 4. Amnesty calls for war-crimes inquiry into shelling in Ethiopia's Tigray

Amnesty International said Ethiopian forces and an allied militia should be investigated for possible war crimes over artillery attacks on densely populated parts of Alamata in September. The group verified 14 civilian deaths in four incidents and also accused Tigrayan rebels of endangering civilians by placing fighters and military facilities in residential areas and schools; the parties disputed or did not answer the allegations.

**Why it matters:** The findings document grave risks to civilians as renewed fighting revives a conflict that killed hundreds of thousands of people between 2020 and 2022.

## 5. Japan accelerates ICC funding before US sanctions take effect

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said Japan will pay its full 2027 contribution to the International Criminal Court by the end of this year. Japan supplies about 15% of the court's budget, and the early payment is intended to give the ICC access to funds before expanded US sanctions take effect in April.

**Why it matters:** Early support from the court's largest contributor could provide a financial buffer and encourage other members to protect the ICC's operations from sanctions pressure.

## 6. US trade investigation raises prospect of new tariffs

The US Trade Representative is preparing findings from an investigation into alleged excess industrial capacity among 16 major trading partners, including China and the European Union, Reuters reported. The prospect that the review could lead to new tariffs triggered a broad sell-off in Asian equities, with technology-heavy markets in South Korea and Taiwan falling most sharply.

**Why it matters:** Fresh US trade measures could disrupt supply chains, deepen economic friction with major partners and add uncertainty for the semiconductor and AI sectors.

## 7. Cyber breach exposes unclassified US intelligence information

The CIA and Office of the Director of National Intelligence discovered an intrusion that exposed some unclassified information on a network used to derive intelligence from public material, a person familiar with the incident told Reuters. ODNI said classified systems and critical capabilities were not disrupted, while the method and full scope of the breach remained unclear.

**Why it matters:** The incident adds to a series of breaches across US federal agencies and shows that unclassified analytical systems can still expose sensitive methods, sources and institutional vulnerabilities.

## 8. Switzerland extends access to emergency aviation-fuel reserves

Switzerland will allow further releases from compulsory aviation-fuel stocks from November 30 as refinery problems, rail constraints and pipeline bottlenecks keep airport supplies tight. About 18% of the compulsory stocks had already been made available since October 15, close to the 20% that officials could release without a departmental decree.

**Why it matters:** The measure is intended to prevent airport shortages as demand rises in December, but it also reveals Switzerland's exposure to cross-border transport and refinery disruptions.

## 9. Swiss committee backs 90% capital rule for UBS foreign holdings

The economic affairs committee of Switzerland's House of Representatives voted 13–12 to require UBS to back foreign subsidiaries with equity equal to 90% of their value. The committee rejected the Federal Council's proposed 100% requirement as well as alternatives of 80% and 75%; the full chamber must now decide.

**Why it matters:** The narrow compromise would materially strengthen loss absorption at Switzerland's only global systemically important bank while reducing the burden sought by the government.

## 10. Switzerland keeps North Korea dialogue open despite closure threat

Switzerland said it would maintain dialogue with Pyongyang after Kim Yo Jong threatened to close the Swiss diplomatic office reopened in October. Bern said it had received no formal demand and that the office's role was independent of sanctions, including measures against a North Korean camp accused of participating in programmes involving Ukrainian children transferred from occupied territory.

**Why it matters:** The dispute tests Switzerland's effort to preserve a rare diplomatic channel with North Korea while enforcing sanctions tied to nuclear proliferation and Russia's war against Ukraine.
