## 1. Trump announces an unconfirmed Russia–Ukraine energy ceasefire

US President Donald Trump said Russia and Ukraine had agreed to stop attacks on energy infrastructure with immediate effect. Neither Moscow nor Kyiv initially confirmed an agreement, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian attacks were continuing while indicating that Ukraine would reciprocate if Russia actually halted its strikes.

**Why it matters:** A verified energy ceasefire could protect civilian infrastructure and create a narrow path toward broader talks, but the conflicting accounts expose how fragile and politically contested the proposal remains.

## 2. Iran rejects latest US proposal as Tehran and Riyadh reopen contact

Iran's Supreme National Security Council chief said the latest US response to a proposal for ending the war and fully reopening the Strait of Hormuz was insufficient. Hours later, the Saudi and Iranian foreign ministers held their first publicly reported contact since intensified Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, discussing regional escalation and possible dialogue between Riyadh and Yemen.

**Why it matters:** The rejection keeps US–Iran negotiations stalled, but direct Saudi–Iranian contact offers a separate channel for limiting a wider regional confrontation.

## 3. Djibouti–Yemen shipwreck kills at least 139 migrants

At least 139 migrants, including 15 children, died and about 84 remained missing after a vessel carrying 305 people capsized near Djibouti on the route to Yemen, according to the UN migration agency. Eighty-two people survived, while search, medical and burial operations continued.

**Why it matters:** The disaster highlights the lethal risks on the Eastern Route as conflict, drought and forced recruitment push people toward Gulf states through war-torn Yemen.

## 4. US judge blocks taxpayer funding for Trump political advertisements

A federal judge temporarily barred the Trump administration from using public money for television advertisements praising the president after a bipartisan backlash. The plaintiffs argued that the spots were political propaganda intended to benefit Republican candidates before the November 3 midterm elections rather than public-service announcements.

**Why it matters:** The order tests legal limits on using government resources for partisan messaging during an election campaign.

## 5. India protests spread as court releases youth-movement leaders

Police detained hundreds of protesters in Delhi for a second day as demonstrations demanding the election chief's resignation also appeared in Mumbai, Kolkata, Panaji and Pune. A court ordered the release of Cockroach movement founder Abhijeet Dipke and two other leaders on the condition that they maintain public peace.

**Why it matters:** The geographic spread of the protests and judicial intervention keep pressure on India's election administration while testing the government's response to a youth-led opposition movement.

## 6. North Korea launches multiple ballistic missiles

North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles of different types from near Pyongyang toward its eastern waters, according to South Korea's military. One travelled more than 900 kilometres, while Japan detected at least two missiles landing outside its exclusive economic zone and reported no damage.

**Why it matters:** The launches extend North Korea's weapons-testing campaign and could accelerate an emerging regional contest in hypersonic and missile-defence capabilities.

## 7. War and debt dominate opening of IMF–World Bank meetings

Officials gathering in Bangkok for the IMF and World Bank annual meetings face the combined effects of the Middle East war, the energy shock, high interest rates and public debt projected to exceed global GDP before 2030. Developing countries face about $400 billion in external debt payments in 2026, while US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is skipping the gathering.

**Why it matters:** Decisions on lending, debt and emergency policy will shape how vulnerable economies absorb simultaneous energy, food, climate and financing shocks.

## 8. Amazon reportedly pursues $7 billion acquisition of Decart

Amazon is in talks to acquire AI startup Decart for about $7 billion, the Wall Street Journal reported, though negotiations could still fail. Decart develops real-time video model Lucy and the Oasis simulated-environment model for training and testing robotics and autonomous-driving systems.

**Why it matters:** A deal would give Amazon advanced generative-video and simulation capabilities while further concentrating frontier-AI companies inside large technology platforms.

## 9. United States reverses restrictions on British AI safety testing

The Trump administration has reversed a request that discouraged American developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic from sharing unreleased models with Britain's AI Security Institute, the Telegraph reported. The change gives developers greater latitude to cooperate on pre-release testing of advanced capabilities and misuse risks.

**Why it matters:** Restoring cross-border model evaluation strengthens a major international safety channel at a time when US policy has otherwise favoured voluntary industry oversight.

## 10. Drone attacks disable a third major Yandex data centre

Yandex said a drone attack completely shut its data centre in Russia's Vladimir region, making some services unavailable and marking the company's third major facility disruption in several days. Earlier strikes hit centres in Kaluga and Sasovo, where two of the three supercomputers used to develop Yandex's AI model are housed.

**Why it matters:** The campaign extends the Ukraine war into civilian digital and AI infrastructure, creating operational disruption while raising questions about the status and protection of dual-use computing facilities.
