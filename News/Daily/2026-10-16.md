## 1. Trump says the United States is discussing denuclearization with Russia and China

US President Donald Trump said Washington was talking with Moscow and Beijing about denuclearization, arguing that the three powers should reduce their nuclear arsenals. Russia retains the largest overall stockpile, the United States has the most deployed warheads, and China is expanding its arsenal, while the New START treaty expired earlier in 2026.

**Why it matters:** Any credible three-way restraint could reshape strategic stability after the lapse of the last major US-Russian nuclear-arms treaty.

**Why it matters:** The exchange raises the diplomatic temperature around contested waters where confrontations already carry a risk of broader regional escalation.

## 3. Lula pledges free weekend public transport before Brazil's runoff

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva proposed a constitutional amendment providing free public transport at weekends, funded through federal budget allocations. The voter-friendly measure came as Lula unexpectedly trailed Flávio Bolsonaro before the October 25 presidential runoff and prompted renewed concern about fiscal discipline.

**Why it matters:** The proposal could affect turnout and campaign dynamics while adding another spending commitment during a closely contested national election.

## 5. Switzerland releases aviation-fuel reserves for Zurich Airport

The Federal Council authorised withdrawals of up to 30,000 cubic metres from Switzerland's compulsory aviation-fuel stocks through November 1, equivalent to about 6.5% of the reserve. Rail disruptions on feeder routes in Germany and low Rhine water levels had diverted freight and created a supply bottleneck at Zurich Airport.

**Why it matters:** The intervention demonstrates how transport disruptions and climate-related river conditions can quickly test Switzerland's strategic supply resilience.

## 6. RUAG and Lockheed Martin expand Swiss F-35A collaboration

RUAG and Lockheed Martin agreed that three F-35A aircraft would be assembled and tested at RUAG's Emmen facility, one of four final-assembly locations worldwide. Components are expected in the first half of 2027, with production planned to start that autumn as Switzerland develops domestic maintenance expertise.

**Why it matters:** The arrangement links Switzerland's fighter procurement to local industrial capability and greater long-term autonomy in aircraft maintenance.

## 7. Cassis discusses Armenia-Azerbaijan peace process in Yerevan

Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis met Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan and Foreign Minister Ararat Mirzoyan to discuss bilateral relations, European security and the Armenia-Azerbaijan peace process. ## 9. Financing strain tests the AI data-centre construction boom

Rising interest rates and a market selloff led several AI data-centre developers to delay or reconsider fundraising plans as investors scrutinised debt, power access and execution risk more closely. **Why it matters:** Tighter funding conditions could slow the physical build-out on which continued growth in advanced AI services depends.

## 10. Researchers say online advertising infrastructure enabled a global spying operation

Citizen Lab and Confiant reported that Israeli company Discover-Tech used targeted online advertisements to track users and support delivery of Candiru spyware. The researchers said the campaign reached about 120,000 websites and 75,000 apps and touched sensitive networks including the United Nations, NATO, the European Commission and the International Criminal Court; advertising platforms subsequently removed or suspended associated accounts.

**Why it matters:** The findings show how ordinary advertising systems can be repurposed for surveillance and spyware targeting at global scale.

**Topics:** [Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Cybersecurity.md), [Israel](../../Topics/Israel.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/israeli-firm-used-online-ads-run-vast-spy-operation-researchers-say-2026-10-15/)
