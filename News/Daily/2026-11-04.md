## 1. Democrats make major gains in US midterm elections

Democrats moved close to winning both chambers of Congress, holding competitive Senate seats and flipping several Republican-held seats as counting continued. Jon Ossoff retained Georgia for the party, while exit polling showed broad demand for stronger congressional checks on President Donald Trump.

**Why it matters:** Democratic control of either chamber would reshape the final two years of Trump's term through stronger oversight, investigations and limits on the administration's legislative agenda.

Ukrainian rescuers said the death toll from Russian attacks on an aid-distribution site and shelter in Komyshuvakha had risen to 31, with dozens of people still missing. Russia also continued attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure as emergency workers searched the ruins for a second night.

**Why it matters:** The higher toll and continuing energy strikes deepen the humanitarian cost of the war days before planned US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi.

## 4. Greenland ratifies expanded security agreement with United States

Greenland's parliament approved a security agreement with the United States and Denmark by 16 votes to none, with seven abstentions. The deal permits two additional US bases and the proposed Golden Dome missile-defence system while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination.

**Why it matters:** The agreement expands the US military role in the strategically important Arctic after months of tension over Trump's threats to acquire Greenland.

## 5. Spain's transport minister resigns after fatal rail crash

Spanish Transport Minister Óscar Puente resigned six days after a high-speed train collision south of Madrid killed 69 people. He announced the decision after hours of testimony to lawmakers, saying a minister could not remain after two accidents of comparable seriousness within a year.

**Why it matters:** The resignation intensifies scrutiny of the safety and governance of Spain's extensive high-speed rail system.

## 6. Indonesia detains three police generals over alleged wiretapping

Indonesian police detained three senior generals suspected of illegally intercepting communications belonging to President Prabowo Subianto and other officials, politicians, journalists, activists and businesspeople. Investigators allege the officers bypassed required procedures and later ordered files deleted to conceal evidence.

**Why it matters:** The case exposes a potentially serious abuse of state surveillance at the highest levels of Indonesia's police and government.

## 7. Brazil's Senate approves major anti-gang amendment

Brazil's Senate unanimously approved a constitutional amendment creating a public-security ministry, strengthening coordination and allocating more resources against major criminal organisations. The measure also mandates maximum-security imprisonment for gang leaders and expands asset seizures, while senators rejected lowering the age of criminal responsibility.

**Why it matters:** Organised crime was a central election issue, and the amendment gives President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva broader federal tools against gangs that have expanded across Brazil.

**Topics:** [Brazil](../../Topics/Brazil.md)

## 8. EU court adviser says Gemini answers may infringe publishers' rights

An adviser to the EU's highest court said Google's Gemini chatbot may infringe publishers' copyright when it reproduces recognisable expression or circumvents technical barriers to answer users. The opinion distinguished chatbot answers from training on publicly available material, which it said can fall under the text-and-data-mining exception.

**Why it matters:** A ruling following the opinion could require AI services to compensate publishers and define how European copyright applies when chatbots substitute for visits to news sites.

## 9. European companies plan sovereign AI computing service

French supercomputer maker Bull and Schwarz Digits, the technology arm of Lidl owner Schwarz Group, agreed to develop AI computing services operating entirely under European jurisdiction. Bull will manufacture the equipment while Schwarz's STACKIT cloud will provide computing, storage and AI software to companies and public bodies.

**Why it matters:** The partnership turns European digital-sovereignty ambitions into concrete infrastructure intended to reduce dependence on non-European cloud and AI providers.

## 10. Four in ten Swiss residents use AI for health enquiries

The CSS Health Study found that 42% of people in Switzerland use AI chatbots when experiencing symptoms, more than double the share one year earlier. Usage reached 62% among young adults and 30% among people over 65, although respondents generally treated chatbots as an additional source rather than a replacement for doctors.

**Why it matters:** Rapid adoption makes the reliability and governance of AI-generated health advice an immediate public-health issue in Switzerland.
