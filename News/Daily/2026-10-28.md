## 1. Israel's election ends without a clear governing majority

Exit polls and early official counting indicated that neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition led by Gadi Eisenkot had secured the 61 seats needed for a Knesset majority. Arab-led parties performed strongly enough to become potential kingmakers, although both leading blocs had ruled out formal partnerships with them.

**Why it matters:** The result begins potentially lengthy coalition negotiations while Israel remains engaged in regional conflicts and deeply divided over Netanyahu's leadership.

## 2. Qatar tells Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to sustain mediation

Qatar said Iran must stop threatening Gulf states and reopen the Strait of Hormuz to demonstrate good intentions after Qatari mediators visited Tehran in coordination with the United States. The demand followed talks between Iranian officials and representatives of Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan, while Tehran maintained its conditions for ending the war.

**Why it matters:** Qatar's public conditions define a concrete test for diplomacy and link any political de-escalation directly to restoring a critical global shipping route.

## 3. Houthis claim an attack on Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery

Yemen's Houthis said they struck the Ras Tanura refinery with a ballistic missile and caused a fire, while Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the claim. The Saudi-led coalition separately said it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones targeting two regions of the kingdom.

**Why it matters:** Ras Tanura is central to Saudi oil exports, so even an unconfirmed strike raises the economic stakes of the expanding Saudi-Houthi front and undercuts simultaneous peace efforts.

## 4. Russia and Ukraine begin a local humanitarian ceasefire in Oleshky

Russia and Ukraine began a regional ceasefire around the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky to permit aid deliveries and civilian evacuations. Both sides confirmed they were observing the arrangement locally even as strikes continued elsewhere, including against Kyiv.

**Why it matters:** The narrow truce offers a practical test of whether humanitarian agreements can be implemented ahead of planned trilateral peace talks in November.

## 5. Finnish and Norwegian leaders make rare joint call to Putin

Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre spoke with Russian President Vladimir Putin for about 90 minutes and then contacted Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. They urged Putin to end the war and linked the initiative to planned talks among Russia, Ukraine and the United States in Abu Dhabi next month.

**Why it matters:** The call reopens a direct European channel to Moscow while asserting that Ukraine and Europe must participate in any settlement shaped by Washington and Russia.

## 6. ASEAN allies seek reassurance over US Indo-Pacific commitments

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth arrived for an ASEAN defence gathering in the Philippines as regional governments sought clarity about whether the Iran war was reducing American military readiness in Asia. The talks also come amid escalating friction between the Philippines and China in the South China Sea, including Manila's monitoring of China's Liaoning carrier group.

**Why it matters:** Simultaneous pressure in the Middle East and Pacific is testing the credibility of US alliance commitments and the strategic assumptions of Southeast Asian partners.

## 7. US Supreme Court leaves immigration judges unable to pause deportation cases

The US Supreme Court declined to reinstate a rule that allowed immigration judges to temporarily shelve deportation proceedings. The order leaves in place an agreement between the Trump administration and Texas that ended the once-common practice, after cities and a legal-services group were denied permission to defend the rule.

**Why it matters:** The decision removes a form of judicial discretion often used while migrants pursue other legal relief and strengthens the administration's accelerated deportation policy.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

## 8. Swiss cyber authority warns of renewed WhatsApp account theft

Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre reported a significant increase in scams that hijack WhatsApp accounts through fake requests to vote in online competitions. The authority received more than 50 reports in October, many involving successful takeovers after victims entered codes or scanned QR codes on fraudulent sites.

**Why it matters:** The campaign shows how attackers exploit trusted contacts and routine authentication steps, creating a scalable threat to Swiss consumers without needing sophisticated technical vulnerabilities.

## 9. Oracle expands AI infrastructure across Nvidia and rival chips

Oracle said it would expand Nvidia Vera Rubin systems to multi-gigawatt scale while also offering Google's tensor processing units and inference hardware from Cerebras and SambaNova. Some Nvidia-based systems will use Oracle's own Acceleron networking chips, while the company maintained a fiscal-2027 capital-spending target of $90 billion to $95 billion.

**Why it matters:** The plan reinforces the scale of AI infrastructure investment while showing that inference computing is developing into a more diverse market than Nvidia-dominated model training.

## 10. Anduril and Qualcomm plan US-made computers for defence customers

Anduril and Qualcomm announced rugged BlueChip laptops for government and military users, with circuit-board assembly planned at Anduril's Ohio facility and Snapdragon X chips produced by TSMC in Arizona. The companies aim to establish a domestic supply chain, while the laptops are designed to run sensitive AI workloads locally rather than sending data to cloud services.

**Why it matters:** The project combines defence procurement, semiconductor industrial policy and edge AI in an effort to reduce supply-chain and data-sovereignty risks.
