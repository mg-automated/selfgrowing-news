## 1. Bank of Japan raises policy rate to a 31-year high

The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1% to 1.25% in a 7–2 decision, taking borrowing costs to their highest level in 31 years. Governor Kazuo Ueda said the bank's focus had shifted toward stabilising underlying inflation near 2%, while the yen initially weakened as investors focused on two dovish dissents and the absence of more forceful guidance.

**Why it matters:** Japan is moving further away from decades of ultra-low rates as energy costs and broader inflation pressures affect major economies, with implications for global capital flows and the yen.

## 2. Iran says it struck and detained a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they struck and detained the Togo-flagged tanker *Trend* after what they described as an illegal attempt to transit the Strait of Hormuz. The claim came as commercial traffic through the strategic waterway remained far below its recent average amid continuing conflict and threats to shipping.

**Why it matters:** The strait is a critical energy route, so attacks or detentions there can disrupt oil and gas supplies, raise transport and insurance costs, and intensify the wider regional conflict.

## 4. Voting begins in Russia's first parliamentary election since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine

Russia began three days of voting for the 450-seat State Duma in an election President Vladimir Putin has presented as a measure of support for the war. United Russia is expected to retain control amid tight political restrictions, the exclusion of the anti-war Yabloko party from the party ballot, and voting organised in occupied Ukrainian territories.

**Why it matters:** The election will reinforce the Kremlin's institutional control while offering signals about domestic war fatigue, even though meaningful opposition has largely been removed.

## 5. Mediterranean crossings fall, but migrant deaths rise

The International Organization for Migration reported that sea arrivals to Europe fell by about 39%, from roughly 98,000 at the same point in 2025 to around 60,000 in 2026. At least 2,292 people have nevertheless died or gone missing on the routes, up from 1,999 a year earlier, as migrants increasingly use longer and more dangerous journeys.

**Why it matters:** Falling arrival numbers do not mean migration routes are safer; the data intensifies pressure on European governments to combine border policy with legal pathways and effective maritime protection.

**Topics:** [European Migration](../../Topics/European-Migration.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/migrant-sea-crossings-fall-become-more-deadly-iom-says-2026-09-17/), [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/fa512b3ac2a31b8c4a79ae0e981ec94c)

## 6. Swiss upper house postpones decision on UBS capital rules

Switzerland's Council of States postponed until the following week a vote on tougher capital requirements developed after Credit Suisse's collapse. UBS chairman Colm Kelleher said the bank could accept a committee compromise using a mix of Common Equity Tier 1 and Additional Tier 1 capital, but warned that rules undermining competitiveness could force UBS to reconsider its Swiss base.

**Why it matters:** The outcome will shape how Switzerland balances taxpayer protection and financial stability against the competitiveness of its only remaining global systemically important bank.

## 7. OpenAI launches an AI platform for legal work

OpenAI released Astra for Law, a legal-sector version of GPT-6 Astra designed for research, drafting and custom legal applications. The platform combines the model with an index of US legal materials and is initially being offered through a selected-firm programme, with integrations planned for established legal software providers.

**Why it matters:** Specialised professional AI is moving from general-purpose assistants toward domain-specific platforms that could reshape legal research, software competition and the economics of legal services.

## 9. Huawei highlights a widening US-China divide over frontier-AI risk

Huawei rotating chairman Eric Xu said Chinese developers may not yet operate models powerful enough to encounter the frontier risks reported by leading US laboratories and argued that development should continue while risks are managed.
