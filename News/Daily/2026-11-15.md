## Overview

Diplomatic proposals sharpened around Ukraine, Iran and Myanmar, but each exposed fundamental disagreements that make rapid settlements unlikely. ## 1. US negotiators raise Donbas withdrawal in exchange for Ukrainian NATO membership

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said US negotiators had raised the possibility of Ukraine withdrawing from Donbas in exchange for NATO membership, the first time that link had surfaced in the current talks. Zelenskiy rejected conceding territory without a comprehensive package and said any such proposal would require security guarantees, a strong Ukrainian military, reconstruction funding and a national referendum.

**Why it matters:** The proposal combines two of the war's hardest issues—territory and NATO membership—and reveals the scale of compromise being explored despite entrenched Russian and Ukrainian positions.

## 2. Trump considers withholding new Iran strikes absent further provocation

President Donald Trump told advisers he may refrain from further attacks on Iran unless Tehran or the Houthis provoke the United States, according to Reuters. Saudi Arabia and Turkey urged restraint, while US intelligence assessments said sanctions and the naval blockade were damaging Iran's economy but renewed strikes were unlikely to change Tehran's negotiating position.

**Why it matters:** The deliberations lower the immediate risk of escalation while leaving military options open and linking restraint to the actions of Iran and allied groups.

**Topics:** [Iran](../../Topics/Iran.md), [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md), [Strait of Hormuz](../../Topics/Strait-of-Hormuz.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trump-tells-advisers-he-may-hold-off-iran-attacks-barring-new-provocations-2026-11-14/)

## 3. China resists the agenda for a proposed Trump–Xi–Putin meeting

Negotiations over a proposed meeting in Shenzhen among Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin had not produced an agreed agenda or format. China remained reluctant to discuss trilateral nuclear arms control because its arsenal is much smaller than those of the United States and Russia, while officials also indicated that Ukraine was unlikely to be handled in the three-way forum.

**Why it matters:** The conflicting goals of the three leaders make a grand bargain unlikely and have raised concern among countries that fear major powers could negotiate over their interests without them.

**Topics:** [China](../../Topics/China.md), [Russia](../../Topics/Russia.md), [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/new-yalta-trump-putin-push-meeting-that-xi-is-wary-hosting-2026-11-14/)

## 4. ASEAN says it is not relaxing pressure on Myanmar

ASEAN's special envoy said the bloc was not easing its Five-Point Consensus on Myanmar, although proposals about the military-backed government's participation in high-level meetings remained under discussion. Leaders meeting in Manila from November 15 to 17 are due to review Myanmar's exclusion amid pressure from members that have increased engagement with the government.

**Why it matters:** ASEAN's decision will test whether the bloc can maintain leverage over Myanmar while preserving unity among members with increasingly different approaches.

**Topics:** [Myanmar](../../Topics/Myanmar.md), [Pacific Security](../../Topics/Pacific-Security.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/asean-not-easing-myanmar-approach-blocs-special-envoy-says-2026-11-14/)

## 5. Router installation is blamed for the US aviation shutdown

US officials traced the nationwide air-traffic disruption to at least one router installed by contractor L3Harris, which overloaded communications networks serving 17 of the FAA's 22 en-route control centres. Airlines cancelled more than 6,400 flights and delayed more than 8,000 on Saturday, and the FAA expected disruption to continue until late Sunday while it tested for further risks.

**Why it matters:** The finding shifts attention from cyberattack fears to weaknesses in change control and ageing infrastructure capable of disrupting travel on a national scale.

**Topics:** [United States](../../Topics/United-States.md), [Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Cybersecurity.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/thousands-us-flights-canceled-delayed-air-travel-system-recovers-shutdown-2026-11-14/)

## 6. Microsoft reports consumer-service recovery after global outage

Microsoft said its consumer products and services had fully recovered, while Microsoft 365 enterprise systems showed sustained but incomplete recovery. The company continued monitoring an email backlog and said the enterprise incident would not be considered resolved until the accumulated messages were processed.

**Why it matters:** The prolonged recovery illustrates how failures at a dominant cloud provider can persist even after core systems return and can leave businesses dependent on delayed data processing.

**Topics:** [Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Cybersecurity.md), [AI Infrastructure](../../Topics/AI-Infrastructure.md)

**Sources:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/microsoft-says-consumer-products-services-recovered-still-monitoring-enterprise-2026-11-14/)

## 7. Swiss Post and Swisscom plan shared e-voting infrastructure

Swiss Post said Swisscom will operate selected public-information elements of its e-voting infrastructure from 2028, including referendum questions, dates and candidate details. Swiss Post will retain sensitive components such as electronic ballot boxes, verifiability systems, software and source code; voter information will remain anonymous and ballots encrypted.

**Why it matters:** The arrangement distributes operational responsibility between two state-linked companies while attempting to preserve the secrecy and auditability required for electronic voting.

**Topics:** [Swiss Direct Democracy](../../Topics/Swiss-Direct-Democracy.md), [Swiss Cybersecurity](../../Topics/Swiss-Cybersecurity.md)

## 9. Swiss victim-support helpline records more than 11,000 calls

Switzerland's national 142 victim-support helpline received more than 11,000 calls between its May launch and the end of September, averaging roughly 80 enquiries a day. The free confidential service implements a provision of the Istanbul Convention and routes callers to cantonal services or partner organisations.

**Why it matters:** The early demand provides a first measure of nationwide use and highlights the importance of accessible support for victims of violence.

## 10. Zurich expands support for people leaving the sex trade

Canton Zurich awarded three-year service agreements to Heartwings, Solidara Zürich-Isla Victoria and Rahab Zürich to support people seeking to leave the sex industry. Each organisation will receive CHF80,000 annually, while the canton will continue supporting a mainly volunteer-run programme in Winterthur.

**Why it matters:** The agreements turn existing assistance into a more stable cantonal programme and expand practical exit support for a vulnerable population.
