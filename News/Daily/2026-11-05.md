## 1. Democrats secure both chambers in sweeping US midterm victory

Democrats won broad majorities in both chambers of Congress, converting the previous day's early gains into a decisive end to unified Republican control. President Donald Trump acknowledged that the Iran war and high fuel prices had hurt his party, while Democratic leaders outlined an agenda focused on living costs, healthcare and oversight of the administration.

**Why it matters:** The result creates divided government from January 2027 and gives Congress substantially greater power to investigate, constrain and negotiate with the Trump administration.

## 2. Israel begins coalition process after election results are certified

President Isaac Herzog accepted the certified results of Israel's October 27 election and began consultations on who should attempt to form a government. The elections commission said it found no irregularities that compromised the vote, in which Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc lost its parliamentary majority and Arab-led parties made historic gains.

**Why it matters:** The consultations could bring Netanyahu's opponents to power, produce an unusual cross-bloc coalition or lead Israel back to another election if no majority can be assembled.

## 3. Death toll from Russian attack on Ukrainian aid site reaches 40

Ukraine said 40 people were now confirmed dead after Russia struck a school being used as an aid-distribution site and shelter in Komyshuvakha. The higher toll is a material update to the 31 deaths reported a day earlier as rescuers continued searching the site.

**Why it matters:** The rising civilian toll adds pressure ahead of planned US-mediated talks and underscores that intensive attacks are continuing despite renewed diplomacy.

## 4. South Korea expands naval mission towards Strait of Hormuz

South Korea said it would extend the operating area of its Cheonghae anti-piracy unit from the Gulf of Aden towards waters south of Oman near the Strait of Hormuz. Seoul also plans to add a P-8A maritime patrol aircraft, an explosive-ordnance team and unmanned mine-clearing systems.

**Why it matters:** The move is South Korea's first concrete military contribution near the Iran conflict and links protection of a critical energy route to pressure from Washington for allied support.

## 5. China seeks extradition of five Uyghurs held in Thailand

China sought the extradition of five Uyghur men who have been detained in Thailand since 2014, with Thai authorities saying the men can challenge the request in court. Human-rights groups warned that they could face torture or other serious abuse if returned to China.

**Why it matters:** The case tests Thailand's non-refoulement obligations and its willingness to resist pressure from Beijing after the controversial deportation of 40 Uyghurs in 2025.

## 6. European Union offers Serbia a route to restart membership talks

The European Commission said Serbia could make a fresh start in its membership process if the student movement that won the country's election forms a government committed to strengthening democracy. The bloc also indicated that further enlargement could advance in the coming years, with Montenegro described as being on track for membership in 2028.

**Why it matters:** The offer ties Serbia's stalled EU path directly to democratic reforms and gives the incoming political movement a significant foreign-policy choice.

**Topics:** [Serbia](../../Topics/Serbia.md), [European Union](../../Topics/European-Union.md)

**Sources:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/acd61b3edcb7b5da8a21b4558f32831b)

## 7. Swiss government cuts Swissinfo funding by nearly half

The Federal Council approved a 2027–2030 service agreement that reduces federal support for Swissinfo from about CHF18.8 million to CHF10.5 million. The international public-service outlet will close its Portuguese and Japanese services and continue in Switzerland's three official languages plus English, Spanish, Chinese, Russian and Arabic.

**Why it matters:** The 44% reduction narrows Switzerland's publicly funded international news reach and has immediate consequences for language access and media pluralism.

## 8. US government prepares consolidated legal guidance for AI laboratories

Pentagon Chief Technology Officer Emil Michael said the US federal government would set out later in November which laws and regulations apply to domestic AI laboratories. The announcement follows growing tension between national-security demands, AI development and calls for clearer oversight.

**Why it matters:** A consolidated federal position could materially affect how leading AI companies manage safety, compliance and government contracts.

## 9. Qualcomm signs major 5G infrastructure deal with SpaceX

Qualcomm agreed to supply radio-access technology for SpaceX's Starlink Mobile network, with an initial focus on terrestrial 5G infrastructure. The architecture is designed to expand coverage and capacity while accommodating future developments in network intelligence and connectivity.

**Why it matters:** The deal brings a major mobile-chip and radio supplier into SpaceX's effort to combine terrestrial and satellite communications at large scale.

## 10. Democratic sweep triggers $750 billion rally in US technology shares

Shares in Amazon, Meta, Alphabet and Microsoft rose sharply as investors reassessed data-centre investment and possible AI regulation after Democrats won Congress. Reuters estimated that the rally added about $750 billion in market value to major US technology companies in one session.

**Why it matters:** The reaction shows how political control, power constraints and AI infrastructure policy are rapidly being priced into the largest technology companies.
